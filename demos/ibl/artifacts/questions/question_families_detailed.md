## Family 001: Stable decisions under changing population organization

- Shortlist disposition: **Shortlisted for planning; this is not scientific approval**
- Proposal review status: `model_generated`
- Authority ceiling: `provisional_inspiration`

### Scientific background

Tests whether stable decision-related behavior is associated with preserved relational geometry or with context-sensitive reconfiguration of neural trajectories.

### Shared scientific tension

Behavior may remain stable because task-relevant geometric relations are preserved, or because neural dynamics reconfigure while maintaining an effective readout; descriptive geometry alone cannot distinguish these accounts.

### Family structure

- Semantic axes: population_scope; target_contrast; discriminating_observation; outcome_meaning
- Distinctions that should not be merged: Cross-context conservation asks what survives changes among recordings and populations.; Within-episode reconfiguration asks how changing trajectories can coexist with a stable readout during behavior.; The first variant's consequence is contextual generalization; the second's is flexible within-task computation.
- Proposal-stage uncertainties: Whether comparable contextual contrasts exist; Whether geometry and trajectories can be estimated reliably; Whether apparent stability reflects standardized measurement rather than neural organization
- Dataset assumptions: The narrative may allow some cross-context and temporally resolved comparisons.; Exact timing, comparability, and joint coverage remain for downstream verification.

### Variant 001.001: Cross-context invariance branch

- Shortlist disposition: **Active for planning**
- Distinction axes: population_scope, discriminating_observation, claim_level
- Distinct from sibling variants: This variant concerns conservation across subjects, sessions, or laboratories and uses cross-context behavioral generalization as the discriminator; it does not ask whether geometry reconfigures within a decision episode.

#### Question

Across subjects, sessions, and laboratories, is stable decision behavior predicted more strongly by preservation of relational population geometry than by preservation of particular activity patterns?

#### Scientific tension and why it matters

Stable behavior could depend on invariant relations among task states despite changing activity patterns, or it could arise from unrelated compensatory readouts without geometric conservation.

This would clarify which population-level properties are plausible carriers of generalizable decision information while avoiding the assumption that stable centroids or individual-unit tuning define the code.

#### Proposal-stage novelty hypothesis — not a verdict

The adjacent advance is to ask whether relational geometry generalizes across heterogeneous acquisition contexts and predicts stable decision behavior beyond activity-pattern similarity, rather than repeating the close prior within-session geometry–behavior association.

#### Competing explanations

- Relational geometry is preserved because it carries task-relevant information that supports stable readout.
- Behavior remains stable through flexible or compensatory readouts even when relational geometry changes.
- Apparent preservation reflects shared task inputs, measurement reliability, or standardized processing rather than a behaviorally consequential organization.

#### Discriminating observation

Independent comparisons would favor geometric conservation if preservation of task-state relations predicts out-of-context behavioral consistency after accounting for overall activity similarity and reliability; preserved behavior with unreproducible geometry would favor flexible-readout accounts.

#### What different outcomes would mean

- Positive: A positive result would support a predictive claim that relational geometry, rather than specific activity patterns, marks a generalizable population code.
- Negative: A negative result would weaken geometry-preservation accounts and elevate flexible readout or context-specific coding explanations.
- Null: An indeterminate association would imply that geometric preservation and behavioral stability cannot be separated at the available reliability or contextual scale.

#### Ambiguities

- relational population geometry
- activity-pattern preservation
- generalizable population code

#### Planning challenges

- Defining comparable task states without imposing the expected geometry
- Separating biological variation from measurement and sampling variation
- Avoiding reuse of the same observations to define and validate geometry
- Distinguishing cross-context invariance from approximate reproducibility

#### Dataset assumptions

- Task-related neural activity and behavioral outcomes may be comparable across some subjects, sessions, or laboratories.
- A later planner must verify repeated and sufficiently comparable task conditions.
- A later planner must test whether population measurements support reliable geometric estimation.

#### Dataset leverage hypothesis — not a feasibility certification

Multiple sessions and broad task-related neural observations may allow later planning of reliability and predictive-comparison tests, subject to verification of comparable recordings.

#### Competing explanations

- Dynamical signatures are reproducible and add predictive information about decisions or response times beyond static geometry.
- Simpler geometric descriptions capture the stable signal, while fitted dynamics are unstable or redundant.
- Both apparent dynamics and geometry are dominated by event timing, movement, or acquisition-specific variation.

#### Discriminating observation

The dynamical account would be favored if signatures estimated under separated observations reproduce across comparable contexts and improve prediction of behavior beyond reliable geometric and measured-state alternatives.

#### What different outcomes would mean

- Positive: A positive result would justify stronger predictive use of dynamical descriptions while not proving biological implementation.
- Negative: A negative result would favor simpler geometric summaries or caution against interpreting unstable fitted dynamics.
- Null: A null result would show that model reliability or incremental prediction is insufficient to distinguish dynamical and geometric accounts.

#### Ambiguities

- dynamical signature
- reproducible parameter
- simpler geometry
- incremental prediction

#### Planning challenges

- Model non-identifiability and estimator dependence
- Separating reliability from goodness of fit
- Fair comparison with simpler descriptions
- Avoiding mechanistic interpretation of fitted models

#### Dataset assumptions

- Some recordings may contain sufficient temporal structure for descriptive dynamical modeling.
- Comparable observations may exist for reliability assessment.
- A later planner must verify timing, repetition, and coverage before selecting any model class.
