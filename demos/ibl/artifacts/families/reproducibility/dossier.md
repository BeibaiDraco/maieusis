# Reproducibility of noise-correlation statistics and geometry across laboratories

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `sample_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Distinguishes reproducibility of coarse correlation magnitude from reproducibility of task-aligned covariance geometry in a standardized multisite setting.

## Scientific Motivation

Standardized multisite processes may yield reproducible neural summaries, but existing process-level evidence does not establish whether noise correlations reproduce quantitatively. Coarse magnitude and functional geometry may have different reproducibility profiles.

Variant 2: Standardized task observations across laboratories may allow later planning of cross-laboratory generalization tests for sensory-, choice-, or behavior-aligned covariance geometry. ## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: Residual laboratory variation reflects incomplete anatomical matching or targeting quality rather than failure of the neural summary to reproduce. Differences in behavioral condition, subject composition, or session state produce context sensitivity that is confounded with laboratory. Unit-quality, spike-sorting, firing-rate, or unit-class composition differences shift the estimated correlations. Different distributions of inter-unit distance or other pairwise sampling geometry create laboratory differences in the aggregate summary. The estimand is portable, but limited precision makes laboratory and contextual components difficult to distinguish. Variant 2: Common task timing produces similar geometry without a shared functional organization. Analysis pipelines impose alignment or suppress genuine laboratory variation. Behavioral associations differ across laboratories even when geometry appears similar.
