## Why this question matters

Uses temporal and task-condition dependence to ask whether correlated variability is primarily inherited from sensory input or sustained by internal decision and state processes.

The scientific tension is:

Limited sensory input, circuit organization, and internal fluctuations can all generate information-limiting correlations, but they predict different changes in covariance around sensory input and decision formation.

## Variant 1: Cross-stage sensory-covariance propagation branch

### Why it matters

Testing a cross-stage geometric and temporal propagation signature would distinguish a specified input-linked component from the broader observations that correlations depend on stimuli or that shared axes contain task-relevant information. It would narrow the possible origin of information-limiting variability while retaining an observational, stage-bounded interpretation.

### Original and refined question

**Original Question Scientist proposal**

Does correlated variability track the strength and timing of sensory drive in a manner consistent with an input-linked origin?

**Post-novelty revised proposal**

Across graded sensory-evidence conditions, does trial-to-trial covariance propagate from the earliest observed sensory population to downstream populations as the Jacobian-transformed sensory covariance expected from an input-linked mechanism, with an input-locked feedforward lag and without comparable pre-input, movement-locked, or action-plan-aligned structure?

### Scientific stakes

**Discriminating observation**

For each graded evidence condition, the candidate signature is that downstream covariance attributable to the input-proximal period is approximated by J(s)Σearly(s)J(s)^T, where Σearly(s) is covariance at the earliest observed sensory stage and J(s) is the condition-specific mapping inferred from changes in mean population responses. As evidence changes, the predicted dominant covariance axis must rotate or rescale with this transformation, and variance along it must change in the direction predicted from Σearly(s) and J(s), rather than merely increasing with mean activity. The component should be absent before input, appear first at the earliest observed sensory stage, then appear downstream after a consistent positive lag, and weaken after input-linked activity subsides. Support additionally requires that this signature remain distinguishable from offset-like additive modes, mean-aligned multiplicative gain modes, state-linked covariance, movement-locked modes, and later choice/action-plan-aligned axes under the same evidence manipulation.

**What possible outcomes would mean**

- Positive pattern: A condition-specific geometric match to transformed earliest-stage covariance, together with stage-ordered input-relative latency and separation from additive, multiplicative, state, movement, and task/action signatures, would support an input-linked propagation mechanism from the earliest observed sensory stage for a component of downstream correlated variability. It would not establish a peripheral origin, exclusive causation, or a general explanation of shared variability.
- Negative pattern: If downstream covariance systematically violates the predicted transformation or appears without the expected stage-ordered input-relative lag while matching gain, offset, state, movement, or task/action patterns, the specified earliest-stage propagation account would be weakened in favor of those circuit or behavioral mechanisms.
- Null or ambiguous pattern: If covariance geometry or timing cannot be estimated reliably, the candidate transformation is not identifiable across evidence conditions, or the predicted and competing signatures are empirically inseparable, the result would leave the origin of correlated variability unresolved rather than count against input-linked mechanisms generally.

## Variant 2: Preparation-dissociated internal decision-state branch

### Why it matters

Separating correlated variability from rate-based decision signals and preparation-related population trajectories would clarify whether post-input covariance carries distinct information about decision timing rather than redescribing sensory responses or motor preparation. The inference would remain predictive and associational rather than causal.

### Original and refined question

**Original Question Scientist proposal**

Does correlated variability persist beyond immediate sensory drive and predict subsequent decision timing in a manner consistent with internally generated state or decision dynamics?

**Post-novelty revised proposal**

After the input-proximal response period but before, or independently of, movement-initiation dynamics, does a component of correlated neural variability predict held-out reaction-time variation beyond sensory condition, trial history, measured movement and preparation variables, firing-rate states, and low-dimensional trajectory measures?

### Scientific stakes

**Discriminating observation**

A decision-state interpretation would be favored if a correlated-variability component remains identifiable after the input-proximal response period, is present before or demonstrably independent of movement-initiation dynamics, and improves held-out reaction-time prediction after accounting for sensory condition, trial history, measured movement and preparation variables, and corresponding firing-rate and low-dimensional trajectory states. If its persistence or predictive contribution is lost when preparation or manifold-transition measures are included, the preparation-noise account would instead be favored.

**What possible outcomes would mean**

- Positive pattern: Persistence and incremental held-out prediction that survive preparation, movement, rate-state, trajectory-state, sensory, and history controls would support a distinct internally linked decision-state account for a component of correlated variability, without establishing causality.
- Negative pattern: If the covariance effect disappears specifically after accounting for movement preparation or manifold-transition activity, that pattern would favor the preparation-noise interpretation over a distinct decision-state interpretation. If it is confined to the input-proximal period, it would instead favor a sensory-linked explanation.
- Null or ambiguous pattern: If covariance reliability, temporal separation, or covariate measurement is insufficient to determine whether prediction survives the controls, the result would leave decision-state, sensory, and preparation-noise accounts unresolved rather than count against any one mechanism.
