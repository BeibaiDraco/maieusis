# Covariance geometry and alternative population accounts of evidence accumulation

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Connects noise-correlation geometry to competing temporal organizations of decision formation: a stable accumulation axis or a changing sequence of population states.

## Scientific Motivation

Behavior consistent with evidence accumulation can arise from persistent integration along a stable population direction or from sequential, time-varying population states. Shared variability may constrain these organizations differently.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: A stable-looking direction results from averaging rotating or sequential time-local population states. The direction primarily reflects task-relevant sensory features or externally driven shared input rather than accumulated evidence. Action planning, movement preparation, elapsed time, or other measured task progression accounts for its behavioral prediction. Different regions contain distinct accumulation dynamics, making any stable direction regional rather than a common whole-animal decision variable. Substantial movement of the represented state along a fixed axis is mistaken for a change in axis geometry, or ramping is mistaken for evidence of axis stability. Variant 2, not carried forward (rejected operationalization failure): A stable latent choice or accumulated-evidence axis is embedded within otherwise changing population activity. Different neurons have heterogeneous latencies locked to evidence pulses, responses, or other task events, producing an apparent sequence without information transfer. Response-latency variation, pose, movement progression, or reward anticipation generates the observed temporal ordering. Input-driven changes preserve choice information without a reproducible covariance-defined transition mechanism.
