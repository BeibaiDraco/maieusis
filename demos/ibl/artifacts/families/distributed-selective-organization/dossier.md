# Broad versus selective brain-wide organization of decision signals

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `sample_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Tests whether stimulus and choice signals are broadly expressed across recorded populations or selectively organized by anatomical population and population dimension.

## Scientific Motivation

Brain-wide detectability may reflect genuinely distributed computation, repeated local representations, common task covariates, or selective organization obscured by coarse summaries.

## Variant Plans

- Variant 1 (Independent task-content selectivity versus generic cross-population prediction): Do dimensions defined independently by their representation of task variables have preferential cross-population predictive value over matched non-task-relevant dimensions, within-population predictive structure, and shared sensory, behavioral, and population-state covariates?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant tests whether independently defined task content has selective cross-population predictive privilege over non-task, within-population, and covariate references. The sibling instead asks whether representational formats are broadly conserved or anatomically differentiated without requiring predictive relationships between populations. The discriminating observation is Task relevance must be established solely from a dimension's representation of prespecified task variables within a population, using information separated from the cross-population prediction assessment rather than selecting dimensions for inter-population covariance. Selective organization would require cross-population prediction for those dimensions to exceed matched non-task-relevant dimensions, within-population predictive references, and prediction attributable to shared sensory, behavioral, and population-state covariates. Broadly similar prediction across dimensions would favor generic shared fluctuations or common input, whereas no advantage over the matched references would provide no evidence for selective organization.
  - Planned analysis: Trial-aligned spike data, Beryl anatomy labels, task variables, and measured movement covariates support an independently cross-fitted task-relevance definition and matched cross-population predictive comparisons. A later executor must decode the custom BLoSC spike shards, reconstruct times, and perform leakage-safe population modeling.
    - Refined question: Within simultaneously recorded, anatomically defined populations, do dimensions selected solely for cross-fitted representation of prespecified stimulus and choice variables predict activity in another population more strongly than reliability-matched non-task dimensions, within-population references, and models accounting for measured sensory, behavioral, and population-state covariates?
    - Population and scope: Eligible BWM sessions with two insertions and sufficient included trials and quality-labeled units; anatomical populations are formed within session from a preregistered Beryl-level aggregation rule, and inference generalizes over eligible sessions rather than individual trials or units.
    - Data sources: Ephys metadata supplies eid, pid, cluster_id, unit anatomy, quality labels, trial timing, and raw spike-store structure for trial-aligned population activity.; Expected grain: Unit-by-trial time-binned activity nested within anatomical population, insertion, and eid.; Required variables: eid, pid, cluster_id, beryl_acronym, label, trial_id, stimOn_times, firstMovement_times, response_times, choice, contrastLeft, contrastRight, bwm_include; Limitations: Pair-specific unit and trial sufficiency is unresolved until prespecified eligibility checks are run. The shard format requires a new validated decoding and time-reconstruction executor capability. Behavior metadata and features provide trial-keyed task, wheel, and camera-derived covariates for common-input and behavioral-state reference models.; Expected grain: Trial and trial-window features keyed by eid and trial_id.; Required variables: eid, trial_id, signed_contrast, choice_label, reaction_time, movement_time, movement_direction, movement_amplitude, mean_velocity, feature_mean, feature_peak; Limitations: Wheel and DLC coverage is incomplete and will be handled by complete-case sensitivity analyses and modality-availability indicators. Measured covariates cannot rule out all common inputs.
    - Unit of observation: A held-out trial-by-time-bin population score from an eligible source or target anatomical population.
    - Unit of inference: An eligible simultaneous-recording session, with pair-level estimates combined using session-respecting hierarchical or meta-analytic inference.
    - Hierarchy and dependence: Trials are nested in source-target population pairs, which are nested in sessions and subjects. All feature fitting, tuning selection, and prediction assessment are split or resampled at the trial level within session; uncertainty is clustered by session and sensitivity analyses aggregate first to session.
    - Analysis strategy: Restrict to bwm_include trials, quality-labeled units, and preregistered session, population, and reliability thresholds; join ephys and behavior only on eid and trial_id. Reconstruct spike times from shard metadata and arrays, bin or smooth activity in prespecified stimulus-, movement-, and response-aligned windows, and derive population dimensions separately for each anatomical population and discovery split. Define task-relevant dimensions on discovery trials only by cross-validated encoding or decoding of a finite prespecified set of task variables such as signed contrast and choice; do not use any cross-population covariance or prediction criterion in this definition. Create non-task comparison dimensions from the same population and discovery split that fail the preregistered task-representation criterion, matched to task dimensions on dimension rank, within-population reliability, variance, firing-rate distribution, and time window. On disjoint assessment trials, estimate out-of-sample source-to-target predictive performance for each dimension using a fixed regularized model. Compare task-dimension performance with matched non-task dimensions, a within-population prediction reference using comparable held-out source and target subsets, and covariate-only models. Fit nested assessment models that add prespecified sensory variables, choice and timing variables, wheel and camera features when available, and nuisance population-state summaries computed without the tested dimension; estimate the incremental predictive value remaining after these references. Summarize each directional population-pair contrast within session, then estimate the session-level distribution with hierarchical uncertainty and prespecified population-pair strata; do not interpret predictive asymmetry as causal transmission.
    - Candidate estimands: Within-session difference in held-out target prediction between independently task-relevant source dimensions and reliability- and variance-matched non-task source dimensions. Within-session difference between cross-population prediction from a task-relevant dimension and a matched within-population predictive reference. Incremental held-out predictive performance of task-relevant source dimensions beyond sensory, behavioral, and nuisance population-state covariates. Session-level average of each contrast across eligible directional anatomical population pairs, with uncertainty clustered by session and subject.
    - Validation strategy: Use nested cross-fitting so task relevance, matching, model tuning, and final cross-population evaluation are separated. Verify decoded shard times against metadata constraints, verify trial alignment and unit uniqueness, and run synthetic method-recovery simulations before target-data execution to confirm that the pipeline recovers injected selective and generic shared-state patterns without using target outcomes to select the method.
    - Split strategy: Within each session, partition included trials into outer assessment folds and inner discovery and tuning folds; retain all units from a population within a fold, never select dimensions using assessment-fold cross-population values, and use blocked or stratified folds when trial chronology or task-condition imbalance requires it.
    - Diagnostics: Report session, pair, unit, and trial retention at every prespecified eligibility rule. Check task-condition coverage, missing behavioral modalities, tuning stability across discovery folds, matched-dimension balance, and predictive residual calibration. Quantify sensitivity of results to bin width, alignment window, anatomical aggregation, and reliability threshold using a preregistered finite grid.
    - Negative controls: Permute source-to-target trial correspondence within session and assessment fold while preserving task-condition strata. Use matched non-task dimensions and time-shifted source activity as references for generic shared activity.
    - Positive controls: Require the cross-fitted task-relevance procedure to recover above-permutation representation of the prespecified task variable on discovery-validation data before designating any dimension as task-relevant. Require the spike reconstruction and trial alignment pipeline to pass synthetic injected-signal recovery before execution on target comparisons.
    - Alternative explanations: Shared sensory evidence, choice, movement timing, wheel movement, or camera-derived behavior drives both populations. Global or local population-state fluctuations, rather than task content, produce broad cross-population predictability. Unequal unit counts, firing rates, variance, dimension reliability, or anatomical sampling produce an apparent task advantage. Temporal autocorrelation or trial-order structure creates leakage or inflated prediction.
    - Predicted result patterns: A selective-organization pattern requires a positive, stable task-versus-matched-non-task cross-population contrast that also exceeds the within-population reference and retains incremental value after prespecified covariates. Comparable cross-population prediction for task and matched non-task dimensions, or loss of incremental value after covariates and state references, favors generic shared-state or common-input explanations. Failure to exceed the matched and within-population references provides no evidence for preferential predictive organization of task content.

    - Interpretation limits: Cross-population prediction does not establish directed communication, causal transmission, or a communication subspace. Observational covariate adjustment cannot eliminate unmeasured common inputs. The claim is limited to prespecified task variables, selected anatomical aggregation, eligible simultaneous sessions, and the recorded time scales. This planning evidence contains no scientific result.
    - Resource estimate: Requires a new executor module for BLoSC shard decoding, spike-time reconstruction, trial-aligned population matrices, nested cross-fitting, and hierarchical resampling; expected execution is moderate-to-high memory and CPU work performed sessionwise to avoid loading the full spike corpus.
    - Why this serves the question: The plan preserves the decisive contrast: task relevance is established independently of inter-population covariance on separated trial information, then tested against matched non-task, within-population, and covariate references. Its positive and negative patterns retain the invariant's selective-organization versus generic-shared-state interpretation without making a causal claim.
    - Required new skills: Validated BLoSC spike-shard decoding and delta-time reconstruction. Leakage-safe cross-fitted population-dimension selection, matching, prediction, and session-clustered inference.
    - Unresolved planning decisions: Preregister finite eligibility thresholds and anatomical aggregation before inspecting target predictive outcomes. Preregister the finite task-variable set, time windows, regularization family, and population-state nuisance summary before execution. Choose a missing-modality primary analysis and complete-case sensitivity analysis based on declared availability rules rather than observed effects.
  - What would support or weaken it: A selective advantage for independently defined task-relevant dimensions over every matched reference would support preferential predictive organization of task content across populations, without establishing causal transmission or communication. Broad prediction that is comparable for task-relevant and non-task-relevant dimensions, or substantially accounted for by shared covariates and within-population structure, would favor generic shared-state or common-input explanations over selective task-content organization. No reliable advantage over the matched references would leave selective cross-population organization unsupported and potentially unresolved because limited comparability or reliability could also produce a null result.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: Cross-population prediction is selectively concentrated in dimensions whose task relevance was established independently of inter-population covariance. Prediction is broad across task-relevant and matched non-task-relevant dimensions because generic population-state fluctuations are shared. Common sensory, behavioral, or other measured task covariates account for apparent cross-population prediction. Differences attributed to cross-population selectivity instead reflect baseline within-population predictive structure or unequal measurement reliability.
