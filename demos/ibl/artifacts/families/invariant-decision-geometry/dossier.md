# Invariant and reorganized population geometry during decision-making

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Tests whether population geometry preserves task-relevant meaning across naturally varying recording and behavioral contexts, or whether apparent stability and reorganization reflect different functional organizations.

## Scientific Motivation

Population representations may preserve decision-relevant organization across contexts, reorganize while preserving equivalent meaning, or change because sensory, movement, and sampling conditions differ.

## Dataset Leverage

Dataset claim status is unverified; observed-depth label is schema_metadata_inspected. Variant 2: If recordings contain sufficiently comparable task conditions, choices, and behavioral-state measurements across contexts, they may support proposal-stage comparison of full distance geometry, lower-dimensional or temporal organization, and out-of-context prediction without treating successful behavior or context decoding as semantic equivalence. ## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: A conserved decision computation produces relational geometry that generalizes without target-context calibration. Movement kinematics or behavioral state creates shared sensorimotor geometry that only appears decision-relevant. Subject-, session-, or laboratory-specific fitting, supervised calibration, or generic distribution alignment produces transfer despite context-specific neural organization. Shared recording modality, placement, preprocessing, or task contingencies create apparent laboratory transfer that does not survive changes in those features. Limited sampling or unequal measurement reliability obscures an otherwise conserved geometry. Variant 2: The full representational distance geometry and lower-dimensional or temporal task organization genuinely reorganize, yet different population organizations preserve the same stimulus-conditioned choice relationship. Geometric reorganization changes the represented decision content, so a predictor learned in one context no longer preserves the stipulated relationship in another. The apparent geometric change is produced by sampling or measurement instability, neuron-level remapping, drift orthogonal to preserved geometry, or context-linked movement, arousal, and behavioral-state variation. The full distance geometry changes, but conserved low-dimensional or temporal task organization supports prediction, reproducing the organizational-conservation account rather than demonstrating meaning preserved through stronger reorganization.
