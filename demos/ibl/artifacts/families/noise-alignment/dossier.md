# Functional alignment of shared variability during perceptual decisions

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Tests whether shared trial-to-trial variability is scientifically informative because of its orientation relative to sensory or choice-related population structure, rather than because of its overall magnitude.

## Scientific Motivation

Noise correlations may be largely nonspecific fluctuations, or their geometry may selectively align with population dimensions carrying sensory evidence or impending choice. These alternatives can produce similar average correlation magnitudes but different functional interpretations.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: A sensory encoding/likelihood direction, defined from stimulus-conditioned population responses independently of choices and actions, is uniquely aligned with an information-limiting covariance component and therefore predicts lower sensory discriminability. A task-feature direction or action/readout direction, estimated independently of the sensory-likelihood axis, explains the alignment and is associated with preserved rather than reduced coding fidelity. A formally defined mixture of sensory and task/action directions explains the observation because those axes cannot be cleanly separated in the relevant recordings. Any apparent sensory specificity arises because the sensory axis has greater estimation reliability, signal strength, dimensionality, or opportunity to overlap high-variance covariance-spectrum components. Overall correlation magnitude, an established information-limiting covariance measure, generic dominant variance, movement, internal state, finite-sample geometry, or measurement error accounts for the apparent relationship. Variant 2, not carried forward (rejected operationalization failure): The putative component is a common task-relevant axis that simultaneously carries sensory-feature and action-plan information rather than a separable choice state. Incremental prediction reflects report direction, movement execution, posture, or a learned sensorimotor contingency not adequately captured by measured or decodable movement structure. Response-time prediction reflects motor preparation or response initiation rather than variation in decision formation. Trial history, task condition, arousal, reward expectation, or another global fluctuation produces residual covariance with behavior. Finite-sample estimation, axis-definition leakage, or measurement error creates apparent incremental prediction or report-invariant structure.
