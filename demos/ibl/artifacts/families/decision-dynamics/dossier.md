# Population-dynamical transitions from sensation to action

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Examines whether decision-related population activity exhibits a meaningful transition in input sensitivity or instead reflects continuous evolution confounded with movement preparation.

## Scientific Motivation

Similar choices can emerge from sequential decision regimes, continuous single-regime dynamics, or trajectories dominated by impending movement.

## Variant Outcomes And Plans

- Variant 1 (Behavioral-timing consequence of a choice-contractive regime transition): In the dataset’s perceptual-decision population, does trial-wise entry into a choice-specific contractive population-flow regime mark a bilateral loss of sensory-evidence sensitivity and incrementally predict response time after conditioning on evidence variables and movement-onset or kinematic timing?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant requires an independently defined transition into choice-contractive population flow, a bilateral pre/post change in sensory sensitivity, and incremental prediction of trial-level response time beyond evidence and motor timing. The sibling instead asks whether late trajectories are better assigned to choice formation or movement preparation and does not require a discrete transition or changing sensory sensitivity. The discriminating observation is First estimate, independently of response time, each trial’s earliest sustained entry into a population region whose local flow converges toward the ultimately chosen state and is less displaced by subsequent sensory evidence; this choice-contractive flow-entry criterion is distinct from the cited prior’s simplified-model estimate of a coupled dynamical-regime and neural-mode commitment time. Then require sensory evidence to have demonstrably greater influence before than after the estimated entry, with observations available on both sides. Finally, require entry time to improve trial-level response-time prediction conditional on relevant evidence variables and movement-onset or kinematic timing; lack of time-locking to response onset alone is insufficient.
  - Planned analysis: Accepted as a planning route: trial-aligned visual-contrast, timing, wheel, and spike surfaces can support an independently cross-fitted choice-contractive flow-entry estimate, pre/post contrast-sensitivity comparison, and conditional response-time prediction. A new executor capability is required for population-flow inference and its leakage-safe validation.
    - Refined question: In BWM sessions passing prespecified neural and timing-quality gates, does a response-time-blind, cross-fitted earliest sustained entry into a choice-specific contractive population-flow region precede a lower cross-validated signed-contrast sensitivity and add held-out trial-level response-time information beyond contrast, choice, and measured movement timing and kinematics?

Using held-out trials and time bins, compare cross-validated evidence sensitivity before versus after entry with an interaction between signed contrast and the pre/post-entry indicator; use an independent fold or nested cross-fitting so entry definition and sensitivity estimation are not circular. - Variant 2 (Reciprocal conditional attribution of final pre-movement distributed dynamics): In distributed forebrain populations during a two-alternative visual discrimination reported by forepaw-controlled wheel turns, does the final pre-movement population state provide incremental out-of-sample information about perceptual choice beyond measured movement trajectory, kinematics, timing, and effector, or does it instead provide reciprocal information about movement beyond choice?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant asks whether an operationally defined final pre-movement distributed forebrain state carries reciprocal outcome-specific information for choice or movement under conditional controls. It does not test for a discrete transition, changing stimulus sensitivity, or transition timing, which remain the sibling variant's targets. The discriminating observation is Using held-out prediction or representational information rather than simple correlation, compare the incremental contribution of the final pre-movement neural state to choice beyond movement trajectory, direction, kinematics, timing, and effector, ideally where different mapping contexts permit choice variation with matched or explicitly controlled wheel movements. Reciprocally, test incremental movement prediction beyond choice within the same choice and effector. Choice-only incremental information favors choice specificity; movement-only incremental information favors preparation. If the same latent dimensions support overlapping conditional predictions, neither reciprocal increment is outcome-specific, and reproducible residual joint structure remains after conditioning on both measured choice and movement, the data do not justify assigning a distinct decision or motor function.
  - Planning disposition: Rejected operationalization failure. Rejected for this dataset-task pairing: wheel direction is almost a deterministic encoding of nonzero choice, and the available task offers no evidenced alternative report mapping. Conditioning on movement direction therefore conditions on the choice report itself, so the proposed reciprocal choice-versus-movement attribution cannot preserve its intended contrast.
  - What would support or weaken it: Reliable incremental choice information beyond controlled movement features, without a comparable movement-only explanation, would support interpreting the final pre-movement distributed state as retaining choice-specific organization rather than merely marking a choice-to-action transition. Reliable incremental prediction of movement beyond choice, with negligible incremental choice information after movement control, would favor an embodied movement-preparation interpretation of the late distributed state. If conditional choice and movement predictions overlap, neither provides reliable reciprocal incremental information, or residual joint structure cannot be separated into outcome-specific components, the functional assignment would remain unresolved and a shared-latent-state account would remain viable.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: A transition into a choice-specific contractive population-flow regime separates sensory evidence processing from choice stabilization and contributes to trial-level response-time variation. Population dynamics and sensory sensitivity evolve continuously, so any estimated flow-entry time is a convenient boundary without a distinct computational transition. The estimated transition and its response-time association arise from movement initiation or evolving kinematics rather than decision organization. Evidence strength or temporal structure jointly determines the estimated transition and response time, producing an association without an additional transition-related contribution. Variant 2, not carried forward (rejected operationalization failure): Choice-specific account: the final pre-movement distributed population state contains information about perceptual choice that generalizes beyond measured wheel and body movement features. Movement-preparation account: the state primarily predicts the forthcoming wheel and associated body movement, with little choice information remaining after choice-to-movement mapping and kinematics are controlled. Shared-latent-state account: the same residual population structure jointly relates to choice and movement after their measured features are conditioned upon, without separable outcome-specific information.
