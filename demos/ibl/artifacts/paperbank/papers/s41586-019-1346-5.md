## Question the system extracted

What is the dimensionality and geometric structure of the population code for visual stimuli in mouse visual cortex, and what determines the power-law eigenspectrum of population responses?

## The scientific contrast

Whether visual cortical population responses are primarily high-dimensional, as expected from efficient coding, or confined to low-dimensional correlated subspaces, and whether the observed eigenspectrum is inherited from natural-image statistics or reflects a constraint on neural coding.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Efficient coding could produce high-dimensional, relatively uncorrelated responses.
- Neural responses could instead be confined to low-dimensional correlated subspaces, as suggested by previous reports.
- The neural power law could be inherited from the well-known power-law structure of natural images.
- The eigenspectrum could instead arise from properties of visual receptive fields, firing-rate distributions, or other stimulus-independent characteristics of the neural population.

What would tell them apart: Measure population eigenspectra across thousands of natural images and controlled stimulus ensembles, then test whether the power law persists after image whitening, changes with stimulus dimensionality as predicted, and remains under localized or noise stimulation.

## Background and motivation

- Efficient-coding theory suggests that neural codes maximize information transmission by eliminating correlations in natural-image inputs; such codes are high-dimensional and sparse and can allow complex features to be read out by simple downstream networks.
- Other studies have suggested that neural codes are confined to low-dimensional subspaces, with correlated and redundant dimensions allowing robust computations despite noise.
- The structure of the population code remained largely unknown because previous conclusions were based on limited numbers of neurons or stimuli, while the visual cortex contains millions of neurons and the population code is the subject of long-standing theories.

## Why this question mattered

Resolving the geometry of the visual population code determines how neural populations balance high-dimensional information transmission against correlated, smooth, and robust representations. The paper links this balance to whether small input changes produce stable population responses and to the possibility of efficient, flexible coding with robust generalization.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Large populations of neurons were recorded while mice were presented with natural images and controlled stimulus ensembles. The main two-photon experiment used 2,800 natural images presented twice; a smaller set of 32 images was repeatedly presented to enable reliable estimation of stimulus-related variance. Additional stimulus sets varied input dimensionality and image statistics.

- Population: Awake mice, with simultaneous recordings from approximately 10,000 neurons in mouse visual cortex; the methods also describe recordings from eight mice in total.
- Measurements: Simultaneous neuronal activity, Stimulus-evoked population responses, Cross-validated principal-component eigenspectra, Power-law exponents, Trial-to-trial variability, Signal-to-noise ratios, Stimulus decoding accuracy, Receptive-field properties
