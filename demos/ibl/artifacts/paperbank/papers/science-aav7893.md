## Question the system extracted

How does ongoing spontaneous neural activity relate to the mouse's multidimensional spontaneous behavior and to sensory stimulus responses, and can large-scale neural population activity across visual cortex and distributed brain regions be predicted from behavioral video?

## The scientific contrast

Whether ongoing activity and stimulus-response variability are primarily unexplained noise or sensory-related activity, versus structured multidimensional signals encoding ongoing behavior and behavioral state; and whether sensory and behavioral information are mixed in the same neural dimensions or occupy partly distinct subspaces.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Spontaneous activity or response variability is random noise.
- Spontaneous activity recapitulates prior sensory experience or reflects expectations of sensory events.
- Spontaneous activity encodes ongoing behavioral and cognitive variables or latent behavioral state.
- Behavioral information is captured adequately by low-dimensional measures such as running speed, pupil diameter, and whisking.
- Sensory and behavioral information are mixed in the same neural dimensions rather than being represented in partly orthogonal subspaces.

What would tell them apart: If spontaneous activity encodes multidimensional behavior, facial videography should predict reliable population activity, including activity beyond that predicted by running, pupil diameter, or whisking alone; comparisons of spontaneous and stimulus-driven activity should reveal whether behavioral and sensory information overlap or occupy distinct dimensions.

## Background and motivation

- Neuronal populations in sensory cortex produce variable responses to sensory stimuli and exhibit intricate spontaneous activity even without external sensory input.
- Spontaneous activity and response variability have been proposed to represent random noise, recall of prior experience, or ongoing behavioral and cognitive variables.
- Previous work linked spontaneous neural activity to behavior through measures such as running speed and pupil diameter, but the paper considers these one-dimensional measures insufficient to capture the diversity of mouse behavior.
- The brain may produce structured patterns of activity in the absence of sensory inputs, and ongoing activity may be behaviorally driven rather than mere noise.
- Sensory and behavioral information can be represented in overlapping or mixed neural activity, while the paper investigates whether they occupy distinguishable dimensions or subspaces.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question determines whether a major component of ongoing and stimulus-response variability is structured behavioral information rather than unexplained noise, clarifies how sensory and behavioral information are organized in neural population activity, and assesses whether behaviorally related signals are widespread across the brain.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Mice were awake and free to engage in spontaneous behaviors, including running, whisking, sniffing, licking, and other facial movements. Neural activity was recorded during ongoing behavior, darkness or gray-screen periods, and visual stimulus presentation.

- Population: Awake mice, including six mice recorded with two-photon calcium imaging in visual cortex and three mice recorded with simultaneous Neuropixels probes across forebrain and midbrain regions.
- Measurements: Population neural activity, Spontaneous activity, Responses to visual stimuli, Facial motion extracted from video, Running, Pupil diameter, Whisking, Neural variance and shared variance components, Cross-validated prediction of neural activity from behavior
