## Question the system extracted

How do trial-to-trial fluctuations in the population responses of V1 relate to simultaneously recorded V2 population responses, and does the V1-V2 interaction occur through a selective low-dimensional communication subspace rather than through the full V1 activity space?

## The scientific contrast

Selective, low-dimensional V1-to-V2 communication versus broad or full-dimensional coupling comparable to interactions among subpopulations within V1.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- All or most dimensions of V1 activity may participate in predicting V2 activity.
- The apparent lower dimensionality of V1-V2 interactions may result from V2 population activity being intrinsically less complex than target V1 activity.
- Inter-areal interactions may preferentially use the largest shared or dominant fluctuations within V1, rather than a distinct communication subspace.
- V1-V2 interactions may have a structure similar to within-V1 interactions despite having similar overall pairwise correlation strength.

What would tell them apart: If only a subset of V1 dimensions predicts V2, reduced-rank prediction should achieve near-full-model performance with fewer dimensions, and the predictive dimensions should be distinct from the largest V1 fluctuations and from the dimensions supporting within-V1 interactions.

## Background and motivation

- Most brain functions involve interactions among multiple distinct brain areas or nuclei, and visual processing in primates requires signals to be relayed across cortical areas.
- Previous studies related activity between neurons or populations in different areas and showed that trial-to-trial fluctuations can reveal interactions, but these approaches did not establish how the multidimensional activity of neuronal populations is related across areas.
- It was unknown whether all or only a subset of the dimensions of source-population activity are related across brain areas, and which dimensions are involved in those interactions.
- Pairwise correlations and trial averaging can miss structure in population-level area-to-area interactions because pairwise measures ignore higher-dimensional structure and trial averaging removes moment-by-moment fluctuations.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question distinguishes generic shared activity from selective communication between cortical areas. It can identify which population activity patterns are effectively relayed to another area and provides a population-level account of how activity may be selectively routed across cortical areas.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Repeated presentations of drifting sinusoidal gratings of different orientations, with spike counts measured in 100 ms bins and trial-to-trial response fluctuations analyzed.

- Population: Neuronal populations in V1 and V2 of anesthetized macaque monkeys; recordings included V1 populations of 88–159 neurons and V2 populations of 24–37 neurons.
- Measurements: Trial-to-trial co-fluctuations of V1 and V2 population responses, Pairwise correlations, Multivariate prediction of target-population activity from source-population activity, Predictive and private population-activity dimensions, Population dimensionality
