## Question the system extracted

Do neural population trajectories during auditory evidence accumulation undergo a transition from input-driven to autonomous dynamics with a change in neural mode, and does this transition correspond to the moment of decision commitment?

## The scientific contrast

An initial regime dominated by momentary sensory inputs and evidence accumulation versus a later regime dominated by autonomous dynamics and decision commitment.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- A single-mode or line-attractor account in which evidence inputs remain aligned with a stable one-dimensional decision manifold.
- A bistable-attractor account in which dynamics settle toward choice-specific attractors.
- A recurrent-neural-network-inspired line-attractor account allowing input directions and autonomous dynamics that are not aligned with the attractor.
- A sequential two-regime account in which sensory inputs dominate initially, followed by autonomous dynamics in a different neural mode; this account predicts that evidence before the transition affects choice whereas evidence after it does not.

What would tell them apart: A transition in the inferred flow field from input-dominated to autonomous-dominated dynamics, accompanied by a change in trajectory direction, followed by a behavioural reduction in the influence of auditory evidence after the inferred transition time.

## Background and motivation

- Perceptual decision-making is commonly modeled as being mediated by neuronal networks with attractor dynamics, and attractor theories have captured several brain functions and complex temporal profiles of neural activity.
- Although experimental evidence supports a role for attractors in perceptual decisions, the actual population-level dynamics underlying decision-making had not been directly estimated.
- Existing attractor hypotheses and related models did not adequately describe the recorded neural data, particularly the heterogeneous single-trial firing rates and complex population trajectories observed during decision-making.
- The behavioural drift-diffusion model describes an initial evidence-accumulation regime followed by a regime in which the decision state becomes independent of sensory inputs, but it had remained unclear whether this transition corresponds to a neural change of mode and the moment of decision commitment.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question would determine whether decision commitment is represented by a rapid coordinated transition in population dynamics rather than only by stimulus or response timing. It would also provide a neural marker for studying rapid changes in internal brain state and distinguish competing mechanistic accounts of perceptual decision-making.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Rats listened to randomly timed auditory clicks or pulses from left and right loudspeakers and reported the side with more pulses. Neural activity was recorded while the animals performed the task.

- Population: Rats trained to accumulate auditory evidence and report whether more pulses occurred on the left or right.
- Measurements: Spike trains from simultaneously recorded neurons, Neural population trajectories, Input-driven and autonomous dynamics, Decision-variable trajectories, Auditory click timing and evidence strength, Behavioural choices
