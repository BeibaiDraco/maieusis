# Question-formation trace

- Review status: `ai_reviewed`

## Starting background

- Attractor-based accounts propose that perceptual decisions are mediated by recurrent population dynamics, but the population-level organization of such dynamics and their interaction with sensory inputs remain incompletely resolved.
- Behavioral work on temporally controlled, discrete sensory samples supports temporal integration in perceptual decisions and makes the timing of evidence contributions experimentally examinable.
- The PaperCase describes a related unresolved distinction between an initial evidence-accumulation phase and a later phase that may become autonomous, as well as uncertainty about whether this transition represents a change in neural mode and decision commitment.

## Unresolved gap

The provided evidence supports a gap between influential dynamical theories and a direct, population-level account of how sensory evidence drives neural activity over time. It remained unclear whether perceptual decisions are implemented as one continuous attractor-like mode or as sequential regimes in which input-driven accumulation gives way to autonomous dynamics, and whether that transition is the point after which new sensory evidence no longer changes the choice.

## Dataset opportunity

The study combined precisely time-stamped, experimenter-controlled auditory pulse trains with simultaneous spike recordings from many neurons during the unfolding decision, together with trial-level choices and neural population trajectories.

Because the timing and sign of each auditory pulse are known, neural population activity can be related separately to sensory input and to autonomous evolution across the same trial. Simultaneous population recordings make it possible to estimate changes in trajectory flow and neural mode, while behavioral weighting of pulses before and after an inferred transition provides an independent test of whether the transition corresponds to commitment.

## Resulting question

Do population dynamics during auditory evidence accumulation shift from an input-driven regime to an autonomous regime with a distinct neural mode, and does the inferred transition mark decision commitment such that evidence before it affects choice whereas evidence after it has little or no effect?

## Scientific consequence

A sequential input-driven-to-autonomous result would support a mechanistic account in which commitment is a coordinated transition in population dynamics and would distinguish that account from single-mode, line-attractor, or fixed-attractor explanations. If no such transition were found, or if post-transition evidence continued to influence choice, the proposed neural interpretation of commitment would be weakened and the relationship between behavioral accumulation and population dynamics would require a different account. Thus both positive and negative outcomes discriminate among substantive models rather than merely locating a response time.

The question links a behavioral construct—when evidence ceases to influence a choice—to an identifiable change in neural population dynamics. This is scientifically valuable because it tests whether a psychologically defined decision boundary has a population-level dynamical counterpart and provides a principled way to compare competing mechanistic theories without presupposing one attractor architecture.
