# Question-formation trace

- Review status: `ai_reviewed`

## Starting background

- Prefrontal cortex is associated with flexible, context-dependent behaviour, but the computations supporting that flexibility remain unclear.
- Prior decision-making work provides recurrent-circuit accounts of evidence accumulation and choice, while population-analysis work shows that complex or mixed single-neuron responses can contain separable task-related information at the population level.
- Attention-related accounts motivate early selection of task-relevant sensory information, creating a theoretical contrast with mechanisms that select and integrate information within prefrontal circuitry.

## Unresolved gap

The provided evidence supports a gap between accounts in which irrelevant sensory information is filtered early and accounts in which selection occurs within the circuitry that integrates evidence. Individual-neuron responses do not by themselves distinguish these mechanisms, and the observed prefrontal population dynamics were difficult to reconcile with existing selection and integration models.

## Dataset opportunity

The study combines context switching, two concurrently available sensory dimensions, graded sensory evidence, behavioural choice, and recordings from a population of prefrontal neurons. This design permits relevant and irrelevant inputs to be compared within the same task while their population trajectories are related to choice.

Because context changes which sensory dimension is relevant without removing the other dimension from the stimulus, the recordings can test whether irrelevant inputs are absent, represented differently, or present but redirected by population dynamics. Estimating task-related axes and trajectories then makes model-specific predictions about selection, integration, and movement toward a choice representation testable. A recurrent network trained on the analogous task provides a constructive mechanism for interpreting population patterns, although it is a model-based aid rather than direct proof of the biological mechanism.

## Resulting question

What population-level dynamical mechanism in prefrontal cortex implements context-dependent selection and integration of sensory evidence toward a choice, and do the observed population trajectories support early or separately configured selection models, or instead a recurrent mechanism in which selection and integration arise within the same circuitry?

## Scientific consequence

Different answers would change the inferred locus and organization of cognitive control. Evidence for early gating would support filtering before prefrontal integration, whereas persistent irrelevant-input signals combined with context-dependent trajectories would support within-population routing of information. A recurrent mechanism in which relevant evidence moves the population toward a choice-related axis while irrelevant evidence is transiently redirected would show how selection and integration can be coupled in one dynamical process.

The question is scientifically valuable because it connects flexible behaviour to an interpretable circuit-level mechanism rather than treating complex single-neuron responses as isolated representations. It also tests whether population dynamics can provide a common explanation for context sensitivity, evidence accumulation, and choice, offering a framework for understanding computations that are distributed across heterogeneous neural populations.
