# Question-formation trace

- Review status: `ai_reviewed`

## Starting background

- Prior information is important for probabilistic perception and decision-making, but the neural mechanisms that represent it across processing stages remain poorly understood.
- Prior-related activity has been reported in decision-related regions, while other work indicates that prior expectations can influence or be represented in sensory cortex. Theoretical and modeling work further permits a distributed Bayesian-network interpretation in which inference may involve communication across multiple brain regions.
- The selected brain-wide map and data-release works establish a standardized decision-making task and a cross-region recording resource that can support comparisons across neural systems.

## Unresolved gap

The available literature did not resolve whether prior information is localized to late decision-making areas or distributed across early sensory, motor, associative, and higher-order regions, nor how such information might be communicated between regions. Comparisons were difficult because earlier studies sampled limited and non-comparable regions and tasks. A further ambiguity was whether apparent prior signals reflected neural representations of the prior itself or correlated embodied variables such as posture, movement, or eye position.

## Dataset opportunity

The dataset combines a standardized visual decision-making task with blockwise manipulation of stimulus-side probabilities, trialwise variation in stimulus contrast, brain-wide neural recordings, and video-based behavioral measurements. In particular, low- or zero-contrast trials can reduce the contribution of immediate sensory evidence, while cross-registered recordings permit comparison across processing levels and modalities.

Because the task separates block-level prior information from trial-specific sensory evidence, neural activity can be tested for information about the inferred prior rather than only stimulus identity. Brain-wide, cross-registered recordings make it possible to compare prior representations across processing levels and to examine directed relationships between regions. Video measurements provide an alternative-explanation test for whether neural prior signals can be accounted for by movement, posture, or eye-position changes.

## Resulting question

Where and how is prior information represented and communicated across the mouse brain during probabilistic visual decision-making? More specifically, does it primarily emerge in late decision-related regions, or is it distributed across cortical and subcortical processing levels in a network that supports interarea communication, after accounting for stimulus information and embodied behavioral variables?

## Scientific consequence

Evidence concentrated in late decision regions would support a relatively localized implementation of prior-dependent decision computation. Evidence extending to early sensory and other processing levels, together with interarea relationships, would support a distributed account of Bayesian inference and alter how prior knowledge is understood in relation to sensory processing and brain-wide communication. If signals were largely explained by video-derived behavioral variables, the interpretation as a neural representation of prior information would be weakened. Thus, both localized and distributed outcomes, as well as evidence for or against an embodied explanation, would constrain competing mechanistic accounts.

The question links a central computational idea—combining prior knowledge with sensory evidence—to the organization and communication of neural representations across the brain. Its value lies in using a common task and broad measurement framework to adjudicate between otherwise difficult-to-integrate local and distributed accounts, while explicitly testing a plausible behavioral confound. As a PaperBank pattern, it illustrates how a standardized dataset can convert a fragmented literature into a cross-level mechanistic comparison without assuming in advance where the computation is implemented.
