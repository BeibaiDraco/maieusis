## Question the system extracted

What are the population-level neural dynamics underlying perceptual decision-making, and does a transition from sensory-input-dominated dynamics to autonomous dynamics and a change in neural mode correspond to the moment of decision commitment?

## The scientific contrast

Input-driven evidence accumulation versus later autonomous decision-commitment dynamics, including whether the transition between them marks the time at which the animal makes up its mind.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- A bistable attractor model in which dynamics flow toward one of two choice-specific attractors.
- A drift-diffusion-model line attractor in which a one-dimensional decision variable accumulates sensory evidence until reaching a choice bound.
- A recurrent-neural-network-inspired line-attractor model allowing evidence inputs that are not aligned with the attractor and non-normal autonomous dynamics.
- A two-regime account in which an initial input-dominated phase is followed by an autonomous-dominated phase, with a change in neural mode corresponding to decision commitment.

What would tell them apart: Estimating input-driven and autonomous vector fields from simultaneous spike trains and sensory-input timing can distinguish the hypotheses. If the inferred transition marks commitment, sensory evidence before the neurally inferred transition should affect behavioural choice whereas evidence after it should not.

## Background and motivation

- Perceptual decision-making is commonly modeled as being mediated by neuronal networks with attractor dynamics.
- Attractor theories and related neural models can capture computations involved in decision formation, but the actual population-level dynamics underlying decision-making had not been directly estimated.
- Theories of decision-making distinguish dynamics driven by sensory inputs from autonomous dynamics, including evidence accumulation and commitment to a choice.
- Existing attractor hypotheses did not adequately describe the full range of observed neural activity and complex single-trial temporal profiles in the recorded decision-making data.
- A transition from evidence accumulation to decision commitment may correspond to a change in neural mode, but whether that neural mode change corresponds to the animal making up its mind remained unclear.

## Why this question mattered

Directly estimating the population dynamics would test prevailing attractor hypotheses, clarify how sensory evidence accumulation relates to autonomous decision formation, and determine whether a neural transition can provide a marker of the internally timed moment of commitment.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Rats listened to randomly timed auditory pulses from the left and right and reported the side with more pulses by turning to that side; the task required accumulation of pulsatile auditory evidence over time.

- Population: Rats performing auditory perceptual decisions; recordings included hundreds of neurons per session and six frontal cortical and striatal regions.
- Measurements: Neuronal spike trains, Neural population trajectories and inferred vector fields, Auditory sensory evidence inputs, Behavioural choices
