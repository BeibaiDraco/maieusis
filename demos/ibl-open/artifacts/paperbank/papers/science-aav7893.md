## Question the system extracted

Does ongoing spontaneous neural activity in visual cortex and across the brain encode a high-dimensional representation of the animal’s behavior, and how does this behavior-related activity interact with neural responses to sensory stimuli?

## The scientific contrast

Whether spontaneous and trial-to-trial neural variability is primarily noise or stimulus-independent background activity, versus structured high-dimensional information about ongoing behavior and internal state that is mixed with sensory representations.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Spontaneous or trial-to-trial variability is random or Poisson-like noise independent across cells.
- Spontaneous activity recapitulates previous sensory experience or reflects expectations of sensory events.
- Spontaneous activity encodes ongoing behavioral, cognitive, arousal-related, or motivational variables.
- Behavior-related and sensory information are represented in separable or approximately orthogonal neural subspaces, rather than being inseparably mixed.

What would tell them apart: If spontaneous activity encodes behavior, multidimensional behavioral traces should predict held-out neural population activity, including during periods without sensory input, and the resulting behavioral dimensions should be detectable across brain areas and separable from stimulus-specific dimensions.

## Background and motivation

- Neuronal populations in sensory cortex produce variable responses to sensory stimuli and exhibit intricate spontaneous activity even without external sensory input.
- Spontaneous activity and response variability have been proposed to represent random noise, recall of prior experience, or encoding of ongoing behavioral and cognitive variables.
- Much previous work linked spontaneous neural activity to behavior through one-dimensional measures such as running speed and pupil diameter, but mice perform diverse behaviors and ongoing activity may therefore have a multidimensional structure.
- In the absence of sensory inputs, the brain produces structured patterns of activity, and ongoing activity can be as large as or larger than sensory-driven activity.
- Previously reported noise during stimulus presentations may in fact be behaviorally driven.
- Behaviorally related activity may be present in primary sensory cortices and may interact with stimulus responses.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question determines whether apparently noisy ongoing activity contains behaviorally meaningful structure and clarifies how sensory information is combined with motor actions and internal state. It also establishes whether behavioral information is localized or distributed across the brain and whether it occupies dimensions that interfere with, or remain distinct from, sensory coding.
