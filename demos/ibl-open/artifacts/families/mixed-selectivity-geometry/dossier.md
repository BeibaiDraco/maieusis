# Functional forms of mixed selectivity in decision populations

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.

## Question Family

A family distinguishing the representational form of mixed selectivity from its functional relationship to behavioral success and cross-condition readout.

## Scientific Motivation

Mixed responses may provide useful high-dimensional structure, reflect simpler additive mixing, or arise from correlated sensory and movement variables; identifying mixing alone does not establish function.

The discriminating observation is After comparably evaluating mixed/high-dimensional and additive/lower-dimensional representations for task-variable signal, model capacity, and decoding reliability, the key observation would be reproducible incremental prediction of held-out decision success across available condition or session partitions, assessed separately from cross-condition readout. The two endpoints should be classified as uniquely improved, jointly improved, jointly unimproved, or traded off across representation regimes.
  - Planned analysis: Accepted as a faithful predictive-association plan.     - Refined question: Across quality-screened BWM insertion-session populations, do prespecified nonlinear mixed/high-dimensional response models provide reproducible incremental held-out prediction of trial decision success over capacity-matched additive/lower-dimensional models after matching task-variable signal and decoding reliability, and is their cross-condition readout outcome aligned, independent, or traded off?
    - Population and scope: All BWM ephys insertion-session populations with documented spike shards, valid task trials, and prespecified minimum quality-screened unit and trial counts. Primary inference generalizes across eligible insertion-session populations; region-stratified analyses are secondary and require prespecified minimum coverage.

    - Unit of inference: Insertion-session population, with uncertainty clustered and hierarchically partial-pooled across session, subject, lab, and region where coverage permits.

    - Analysis strategy: Define the primary response window strictly before movement onset when timing permits, with a locked stimulus-aligned fallback and explicit exclusion of trials lacking the required temporal ordering. Fit two preregistered representation families to the same population responses: an additive lower-dimensional task model with main effects for signed sensory evidence, prior, choice, and allowed timing or movement covariates; and a nonlinear mixed/high-dimensional model using the same inputs plus locked interaction basis terms. Match effective capacity by parameter-count or degrees-of-freedom targets, identical regularization search spaces, training budgets, response normalization, and nested tuning procedure. Do not assign representation class from correct-versus-error performance. Quantify task-variable signal and within-condition decoding reliability in training data only, then form matched comparison strata or include these quantities as nuisance covariates in the population-level comparison. For the behavioral endpoint, evaluate held-out trial decision success using out-of-sample calibrated log loss and discrimination relative to a task-and-movement baseline; compare the incremental benefit attributable to each representation family at the population level. For the generalization endpoint, train each decoder on one prespecified condition set and test on a disjoint condition set that preserves the target decision variable while changing contrast, prior, or stimulus context. Classify each population and aggregate uncertainty as behavioral-only improvement, readout-only improvement, joint improvement, neither, or opposing directional improvements; report the full distribution rather than a selected subset.

    - Split strategy: Nested grouped resampling: all preprocessing, unit selection, condition normalization, capacity tuning, reliability estimation, and nuisance fitting occur within training folds. Outer folds hold out complete sessions when enough sessions exist, otherwise complete insertion-session populations with subject-blocked sensitivity analysis. Null or reversed paired behavioral differences, especially with retained reliability or readout advantages, would weaken a distinct behavioral-predictive interpretation while preserving other coding benefits.

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: Any incremental behavioral prediction attributed to mixed geometry reflects residual differences in stimulus, choice, or movement information rather than representational organization.
