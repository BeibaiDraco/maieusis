## Question Family

A family replacing correlation magnitude with structural alignment, separating sensory-information and decision-behavior meanings of shared variability.

## Scientific Motivation

Shared variability can be nuisance, information-limiting structure, or behaviorally meaningful population organization; its consequence depends on orientation relative to candidate signal dimensions rather than magnitude alone.

## Variant Plans

- Variant 1 (Decision-specific conditional behavioral-alignment variant): Does the component of shared population variability aligned with an independently defined decision-formation dimension—but not a separately defined movement-execution dimension—provide out-of-sample prediction of both choices and response times beyond sensory-evidence alignment, movement covariates, and overall shared-variability magnitude?
  - Distinguishing test: Unlike the sensory-alignment sibling, this variant separates decision formation from movement execution and requires decision-aligned variability to predict choices and response times out of sample beyond sensory alignment, movement covariates, and total shared-variability magnitude; comparable alignment is interpreted as mixed rather than uniquely decision-related structure. The discriminating observation is The discriminating observation would be preferential alignment of shared variability with an independently estimated decision-formation dimension, distinct from movement execution, whose component predicts both choices and response times out of sample after accounting for sensory-evidence alignment, measured movement covariates, and overall shared-variability magnitude. Comparable sensory, decision, and movement alignment would instead indicate non-unique mixed structure.
  - Planned analysis: The variant can be evaluated associationally using trial-keyed ephys spikes, choices and reaction times, and temporally distinct wheel/DLC movement features. It remains conditional on a new leakage-safe spike-decoding and nested-geometry executor, and on the stated decision-axis operationalization being accepted.
    - Refined question: Across eligible BWM ephys populations, does pre-movement shared population variability projected onto a cross-fitted conditional choice dimension improve held-out prediction of choice and reaction time beyond sensory-evidence alignment, post-movement wheel/DLC execution alignment, and total shared-variability magnitude?
    - Population and scope: Eligible task trials from BWM sessions with a valid ephys insertion, trial timing and choice fields, and usable movement measurement for the movement-axis analysis; inference will be across independently recorded insertions or sessions, not individual trials treated as independent populations.
    - Data sources: BWM ephys trial keys, timing/outcome fields, population metadata, and per-insertion raw spike shards.; Expected grain: Trials keyed by eid and trial_id, with raw spikes keyed by pid and local cluster identity.; Required variables: eid, trial_id, choice, contrastLeft, contrastRight, probabilityLeft, stimOn_times, goCue_times, firstMovement_times, response_times, pid, cluster_id, spike_times_delta_ticks, spike_clusters; Limitations: Simultaneously recorded-unit count and eligible-trial coverage must be screened by prespecified quality and reliability gates. BWM behavior trial features and wheel/DLC movement feature tables joined to ephys trials by eid and trial_id.; Expected grain: Trial-level behavior rows and trial-by-camera or trial-by-window movement feature rows keyed by eid and trial_id.; Required variables: signed_contrast, choice_label, reaction_time, movement_time, movement_onset_time, movement_peak_time, movement_direction, movement_amplitude, mean_velocity, max_velocity, camera, feature_mean, feature_peak; Limitations: Wheel and DLC coverage are incomplete, so movement-axis eligibility and missingness handling must be explicit.
    - Unit of observation: A trial-by-simultaneously-recorded-unit pre-movement spike-count or rate vector, joined to trial-level sensory, choice, reaction-time, and movement-feature variables.
    - Unit of inference: Insertion or session-level population estimates, combined with hierarchical or cluster-robust inference across sessions and animals.
    - Hierarchy and dependence: Fit geometry and prediction within each insertion or session using trial-blocked folds; preserve trial order for block construction; aggregate population-level contrasts with random effects for session and subject or with subject-clustered uncertainty. Never split individual trials from the same fitted population across a geometry-training and geometry-evaluation role.
    - Analysis strategy: Pre-register a pre-movement neural window beginning after the chosen sensory event and ending before first movement with a fixed safety buffer; exclude trials lacking valid timing or required covariates. Within outer held-out folds, residualize or stratify choice coding for signed contrast and probabilityLeft so the decision candidate is conditional on measured sensory evidence and prior block. Fit the decision axis only on inner-training pre-movement neural responses. Independently estimate a sensory-evidence axis from signed contrast and a movement-execution axis from wheel/DLC kinematics measured after first movement; do not use post-movement neural responses to define the decision axis. Estimate a low-rank shared-variability covariance from inner-training trial residuals after prespecified mean-task effects, then quantify its alignment with each cross-fitted candidate axis using normalized projected variance and a reliability-corrected sensitivity analysis. On outer held-out trials, compare nested predictive models for binary choice and reaction time: baseline sensory and nuisance covariates; baseline plus total shared-variability magnitude; and baseline plus decision-, sensory-, and movement-aligned shared-variability components. Treat preferential decision alignment plus incremental held-out prediction of both outcomes as the discriminating pattern; treat comparable alignment or loss of incremental prediction after covariates as mixed or non-decision-specific structure.
    - Candidate estimands: Within-population difference in normalized shared-variability projection onto the cross-fitted conditional choice axis versus the independently estimated movement-execution axis. Out-of-sample incremental predictive utility of decision-aligned shared-variability component for held-out choice beyond sensory alignment, movement covariates, and total shared-variability magnitude. Out-of-sample incremental predictive utility of the same component for held-out reaction time beyond the same covariates. Population-level distribution of these foldwise contrasts, accounting for session and subject dependence.
    - Validation strategy: Before target evaluation, perform synthetic recovery using simulated populations with known sensory, decision, movement, and shared-covariance directions; verify the executor decodes shard timing and cluster identity against documented metadata; require fold-local axis fitting, temporal eligibility checks, and permutation of training-only choice labels to show that the pipeline does not leak outcomes.
    - Split strategy: Use nested cross-fitting within insertion or session with contiguous or block-stratified trial folds to respect temporal dependence. All residualization, scaling, dimensionality reduction, axis fitting, covariance estimation, missing-data imputation, and hyperparameter selection occur inside training folds. The final outcome models are scored only on outer held-out trials; tuning choices are made from synthetic recovery and inner-fold stability, not held-out target scores.
    - Diagnostics: Report eligible populations, units, trials, wheel/DLC availability, timing exclusions, and fold balance as aggregate summaries. Evaluate axis stability across folds, projected-variance confidence intervals, covariance-rank sensitivity, and reliability equality across sensory, decision, and movement axes. Audit temporal separation between neural window, first movement, post-movement kinematics, and response time. Check collinearity and overlap among candidate axes and report whether orthogonalization changes the qualitative comparison.
    - Negative controls: Use fold-local permutations of conditional choice labels when fitting the decision axis; this should eliminate decision-specific held-out utility while retaining the same computational workflow. Project shared variability onto a matched random orthogonal direction and test whether its apparent utility matches the decision component. Use temporally shifted post-trial movement features as a nuisance-control construction that should not create a pre-movement decision-specific result.
    - Positive controls: Verify that the sensory-evidence axis can recover held-out signed-contrast structure from the pre-movement population under the same cross-fitting machinery. Verify that the movement-execution axis predicts post-first-movement wheel/DLC features in held-out trials when those features are available.
    - Alternative explanations: A residual sensory or block-prior axis may masquerade as conditional choice coding. Pre-movement activity may contain movement preparation that is not captured by available wheel/DLC summaries. Unequal reliability or dimensionality of the candidate axes may distort alignment comparisons. Slow arousal, engagement, or trial-history state may jointly affect covariance, response time, and choice. Population sampling, region composition, and session quality may induce heterogeneous geometry.
    - Predicted result patterns: Supportive associational pattern: Decision-axis alignment exceeds the movement-axis comparison under reliability-matched analyses and adds held-out prediction for both choice and reaction time after the prespecified sensory, movement, and magnitude controls. Mixed or weakening pattern: Candidate alignments are comparable, or the decision component has no incremental held-out utility after sensory and movement controls, favoring non-unique, sensory, motor, or generic shared-variability structure. Inconclusive pattern: Foldwise geometry is unstable or coverage gates fail, so absence of incremental prediction cannot distinguish absent association from inadequate geometric estimation.
    - Claim ceiling: associational
    - Interpretation limits: This observational dataset cannot establish that shared variability causes decision formation, and conditional choice coding is not a direct measurement of an internal decision variable. Temporal exclusion and measured kinematic covariates reduce but cannot eliminate motor-preparation, latent-state, and unmeasured-movement confounding. Results must be framed as population-specific associations and may not generalize across regions, tasks, or recording configurations without separate support. Planning evidence documents data availability only and is not a scientific result.
    - Resource estimate: A new executor should stream and decode per-pid spike shards, construct trial-aligned population matrices without materializing the full spike corpus, and run nested cross-fitting per eligible population; expected compute is moderate-to-high and should be scheduled per insertion with bounded memory.
    - Why this serves the question: The plan retains the protected contrast between decision formation and movement execution by defining them from temporally distinct and independently estimated sources, controls sensory evidence and overall covariance magnitude, and requires incremental held-out prediction of both choice and reaction time. It makes comparable alignment an explicit mixed-structure outcome rather than silently reinterpreting it as decision-specific.
    - Required new skills: A BWM spike-shard reader that decodes delta-int ticks and dense local cluster indices into bounded trial-aligned population counts. A leakage-audited nested cross-fitted shared-variability geometry and multi-outcome prediction executor with synthetic recovery tests.
    - Unresolved planning decisions: Owner confirmation of the conditional pre-movement choice axis as the decision-formation operationalization. A fixed neural-window and pre-movement buffer specification selected independently of target associations. A prespecified approach for sessions lacking wheel or DLC features, including whether the primary estimand requires both or permits a wheel-only movement axis with a camera-availability sensitivity analysis.
  - What would support or weaken it: Decision-specific alignment with incremental held-out prediction of choices and response times would support the associational claim that part of shared variability tracks decision formation rather than merely sensory structure, generic variability strength, or motor implementation. If prediction is absorbed by sensory or movement covariates, or if sensory, decision, and movement alignments are comparable, the result would weaken a singular decision-related interpretation and favor sensory, motor, or non-unique mixed structure. If candidate dimensions or their alignments cannot be estimated reliably enough to compare held-out prediction, the functional meaning of the shared-variability geometry would remain unresolved.
