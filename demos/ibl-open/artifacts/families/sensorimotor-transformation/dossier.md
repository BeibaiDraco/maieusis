# Population geometry of brain-wide sensorimotor transformation

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.

## Question Family

A family asking whether sensory-to-choice transformation is organized as invariant shared geometry or region-specific transformation, with separate geometry-invariance and predictive-routing variants.

## Scientific Motivation

Brain-wide evidence and preparation signals may reflect a shared population organization propagated across regions, region-specific transformations, or common task and movement inputs; broad encoding alone does not distinguish these accounts.

## Variant Plans

- Variant 1 (Predictive-routing variant): Do time-varying cross-region population relationships predict the transition from sensory evidence to movement preparation better than independent local representations or common task inputs?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant asks whether time-varying cross-region relationships incrementally predict transformation stages; the sibling concerns cross-region geometric invariance or transformation. The discriminating observation is Cross-region population state information that adds held-out prediction of later preparation-related states beyond local history, task inputs, and measured movement would favor a routing-like predictive account.
  - Planned analysis: The variant remains feasible as a predictive, non-causal comparison in simultaneously sampled region pairs. This revision directly resolves the review blocker by temporally restricting the primary baseline and by treating outcome-realized behavior only as secondary descriptive context, never as a primary common-input control.
    - Refined question: Across prespecified simultaneously recorded region pairs, does an earlier source-region population state improve held-out prediction of a later pre-first-movement target-region state beyond target information, task information, trial history, and measured movement information that were all available no later than the source bin?
    - Population and scope: BWM ephys sessions with prespecified multi-region pair coverage, retained label-1 units, BWM-included trials, and a source-bin covariate-availability tier. Inference generalizes across eligible sessions and region pairs, not to physical communication.
    - Data sources: Ephys trial and event timing, unit region labels, and per-insertion spike-time and cluster-assignment stores. First-movement timing defines the pre-movement target window and is not a primary baseline covariate.; Expected grain: Spike events nested in units, insertions, sessions, trials, time bins, and region pairs.; Required variables: eid, pid, cluster_id, unit quality label, Beryl region label, trial_id, stimulus time, go-cue time, first-movement time for target-window definition only, signed contrast, BWM inclusion flag; Limitations: Only 240 sessions have two insertions, and usable region-pair population size remains to be screened with prespecified thresholds. Spike-store decoding is required before population-state construction. Trial behavior, wheel, DLC, event-aligned behavior, and movement-state products joined on eid and trial_id. Only values whose measurement window ends at or before the source bin may enter the primary baseline.; Expected grain: Trial and event summaries nested in sessions, with camera-specific DLC availability and session-level movement epochs.; Required variables: signed contrast and task-event timing available by the source bin, prior-trial history, wheel direction or velocity summaries restricted to causal-past source-bin windows, available DLC summaries restricted to causal-past source-bin windows, movement-state or quiescence indicators restricted to causal-past source-bin windows; Limitations: Wheel and DLC availability is incomplete and must define prespecified primary and sensitivity tiers. Measured causal-past movement covariates cannot eliminate unmeasured common inputs.
    - Unit of observation: A trial-by-time-bin regional population-state vector, with each source bin preceding its target bin by a prespecified lag.
    - Unit of inference: Session-level and region-pair-level held-out predictive contrasts, combined with hierarchical uncertainty that accounts for repeated pairs within sessions and subjects.
    - Hierarchy and dependence: Neurons are nested in insertion and region; bins are nested in trials; trials are nested in sessions and subjects. Split complete trials or blocked trial groups before model fitting, retain all bins of a held-out trial together, and use hierarchical or cluster-resampled uncertainty at session and subject levels.
    - Analysis strategy: Restrict to BWM-included trials in sessions meeting prespecified simultaneous region-pair and unit-count thresholds, with sensitivity analyses across reasonable thresholds fixed before target evaluation. Decode only required spike shards to create quality-filtered, variance-stabilized, event-aligned regional population states in prespecified time bins spanning evidence and pre-first-movement epochs. For each directed region pair and lag, construct a primary baseline for the later target state from target-region state history ending at the source bin, signed contrast and task-event indicators already realized by the source bin, prior-trial history, and wheel, DLC, movement-state, or quiescence features whose recorded window ends no later than the source bin. Exclude reaction time, final choice, response time, first-movement time, movement time, future task-event indicators, and every wheel, DLC, or movement feature measured after the source bin from the primary baseline. First-movement time is used only to delimit the pre-first-movement target state. Fit a matched augmented model that adds the source-region population state at the source bin. Evaluate out-of-sample predictive improvement on held-out trials and aggregate directed contrasts without selecting lags or pairs from observed improvement. Treat models adjusted for final choice, reaction time, response timing, or post-source movement summaries as explicitly secondary descriptive analyses. They do not estimate the primary temporally ordered contrast and cannot override its interpretation. Use a preregistered directional pattern criterion: temporally ordered, stable source-to-target improvement that survives causal-past covariate and shuffled-time controls favors a routing-like predictive description; null, symmetric, or unstable improvement favors independent-local or common-input accounts.
    - Candidate estimands: Primary estimand: within a region pair and session, the held-out difference in later target-state prediction loss between a causal-past baseline and an otherwise matched model that adds the source population state at the source bin. The hierarchical average of the primary held-out loss difference across eligible directed region pairs and sessions, with heterogeneity reported rather than collapsed into a single routing claim. The contrast between observed-lag primary-estimand improvement and time-shifted or trial-permuted source-state controls matched for autocorrelation and event locking. Secondary descriptive estimand: the same predictive comparison after adding outcome-realized choice, reaction-time, response, or post-source movement summaries, reported only as sensitivity context and not as evidence about temporally ordered routing.
    - Validation strategy: Before target evaluation, verify spike-shard decoding against metadata cluster counts on a small non-target structural sample; test synthetic recovery of a known lagged incremental-prediction signal; verify no trial or time-bin leakage across splits; and audit every primary covariate's timestamp and aggregation window to confirm it ends at or before the source bin.
    - Split strategy: Primary evaluation uses grouped held-out trials within session, with all bins from a trial confined to one fold. Secondary generalization leaves out whole sessions and, where feasible, subjects. Region-pair and lag definitions are fixed from anatomy and task timing before fitting.
    - Diagnostics: Report eligible-session, region-pair, unit-count, trial-count, wheel, DLC, and causal-past covariate coverage after each prespecified inclusion rule. Audit the source-bin temporal-availability rule for every primary feature and report exclusions caused by unavailable causal-past measurements. Assess missingness by session, task condition, and region pair without conditioning inclusion on target prediction values. Assess target-state dimensionality, model convergence, residual temporal autocorrelation, split balance, and stability over bin widths and lag windows. Report directional asymmetry and between-session heterogeneity with uncertainty.
    - Negative controls: Time-shift or trial-permute source population states while preserving within-region autocorrelation and trial structure. Evaluate source-state prediction of a matched non-preparation target window where the proposed ordering should not apply.
