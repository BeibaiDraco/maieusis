# Persistent versus sequential population dynamics during evidence accumulation

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

A family asking whether decision-related population activity is better understood as persistent evidence representation or as choice-selective sequential progression, while separating representational form from behavioral relevance.

## Scientific Motivation

Evidence accumulation is established as a useful account of perceptual decisions, but persistent and sequential neural implementations remain unresolved and may differ across brain populations.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: Apparent sequences reflect sensory-locked latency differences rather than trajectories that carry accumulated evidence. Orderly progression reflects choice commitment or overt movement preparation and execution rather than evidence accumulation. Apparent persistence arises from averaging transient or temporally jittered responses across neurons or trials. Persistent and sequential signatures describe different temporal or population scales of the same organization rather than categorically different regional mechanisms. Regional differences arise from unequal measurement quality or population sampling rather than representational heterogeneity. Variant 2: Either signature may predict behavior only because it tracks stimulus strength or timing. A predictive advantage may reflect movement preparation, event locking, or response execution rather than evidence accumulation. Unequal model flexibility, neural sampling, or measurement timescale may favor one signature definition. A population classified as hybrid may contain distinct subpopulations or trial regimes rather than a single mixed dynamical organization.
