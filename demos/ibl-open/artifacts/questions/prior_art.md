# What prior-art review found, and which questions it stopped

Before a question may be planned it is checked against the published record. This page is every version that check did not let through, with the work it ran into.

A stop here is not a verdict on the dataset and not a fault. It is the review doing its job: the questions that got through are the ones that survived it, and reading this page is how you tell that claim from an assertion.

Every record named below resolves to a DOI. A model's impression that something exists is not enough to stop a question; the prior has to resolve to an identity a reader can open. The prose under each one is the reviewer's own account of the overlap, not a summary written afterwards.

## Temporal origin of choice-predictive population activity — Temporal-origin variant

Deferred: the search could not retrieve enough to judge the question either way. The records below were read and none of them tests what this version proposed.

**Why.** Content-bearing records establish relevant separations among sensory evidence, decision formation, and motor preparation, but none assesses the proposed within-population, independently identified three-component decomposition and its relative stage-dependent shift. The most apparently proximate records concerning the origins of choice-related sensory-cortical activity and comparisons of sensory versus motor-preparatory decision signals are title-only, so their central contrast, controls, and discriminating outcomes cannot be compared. They therefore cannot establish either a direct recap or an adequately supported in-scope absence of one.

**Compared against 5 records:**

- [10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119667](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119667)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern separating decision-related neural signals from motor/action-preparation signals over the course of a perceptual decision.
  - *Same tension.* The prior distinguishes evidence-integration-related alpha/beta lateralization from an LRP tied more closely to motor output; this overlaps the proposal's concern that late choice prediction need not be pure preparation.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior compares distinct motor-cortical signal classes across task epochs in human experiments, rather than estimating sensory-linked, decision-linked, and preparation-linked components within one predefined recorded population.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* It uses whether lateralized signals emerge before an overt response is possible and whether their onsets vary with sensory evidence, contrasted with cue-locked LRP emergence. It does not test a within-population relative-contribution shift after independently identifying all three proposed components or adjusting preparation for movement variables.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* Its positive conclusion supports coexistence of an evidence-related motor-cortical signal and a more response-tied signal. That is compatible with, but does not discriminate the proposal's progression account from its coexistence alternative.
- [10.1523/jneurosci.3355-13.2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.3355-13.2013)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both address temporal relations between evidence-accumulation/decision signals and motor-preparation signals during perceptual reports.
  - *Same tension.* The prior supports a supramodal accumulation process that precedes an effector-selective motor process, bearing on the proposal's decision-versus-preparation distinction but not its feedback-to-sensory-population alternative.
  - *Same contrast.* It contrasts a centroparietal decision-variable signal with an effector-selective motor-preparation signal across distinct signals, not functional components within the same recorded population.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* CPP buildup scales with coherence and reaches a stereotyped report-level amplitude, while the motor-specific buildup significantly lags it. These observations identify temporally ordered signal types, but do not show that relative explanatory contributions of independently defined components shift within a population under sensory and movement matching.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The reported lag supports separate accumulation and motor-preparation processes. Under the candidate's stated interpretation, such independent contributions without the specified within-population shift would favor coexistence rather than progression.
- [10.1038/s41467-017-00766-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-00766-3)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern disentangling sensory and choice-related contributions to neural activity and caution against conflating choice prediction with sensory tuning.
  - *Same tension.* The prior directly supports the possibility that choice-related signals can be separable from sensory inputs and can confound conventional choice-probability interpretations, a relevant alternative to simple sensory-origin accounts.
  - *Same contrast.* It compares sensory-dominant MSTd and choice-dominant VIP activity in monkeys, including passive-fixation tuning for a subset, rather than comparing trial-stage contributions of sensory, decision, and preparation components within one population.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The key prior observation is that VIP choice-related activity is not predictable from stimulus tuning. It does not assess response-locked preparation, action proximity, movement covariates, or an evolving relative contribution across trial stages.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior demonstrates decoupling of stimulus and choice signals, supporting a nonunitary interpretation of choice prediction. It neither supports nor rules out the candidate's specified sensory-to-decision-to-preparation progression.
- [10.1038/nn.4183](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4183)
  - *Same phenomenon.* The title indicates a potentially highly relevant investigation of origins of choice-related activity in mouse somatosensory cortex.
  - *Same tension.* Its title potentially bears on the candidate's sensory-origin versus endogenous/feedback interpretation, but the evidence pack provides no abstract or excerpt specifying the mechanisms considered.
  - *Same contrast.* Population, task, and whether sensory, decision, feedback, preparation, or movement-linked sources were separated cannot be determined from the title alone.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* No discriminating observation, controls, or temporal analysis is available in the persisted evidence.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The outcome and its implications for feedback, coexistence, or a stage-dependent component shift are unavailable.
- [10.1523/jneurosci.0668-18.2018](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.0668-18.2018)
  - *Same phenomenon.* The title indicates a potentially close comparison of decision-related signals in sensory and motor-preparatory neuronal responses in area LIP.
  - *Same tension.* The title potentially overlaps the candidate's decision-versus-preparation distinction, but does not reveal whether it evaluates temporal progression, feedback, or coexisting signals.
  - *Same contrast.* Whether the prior studies one population, how sensory and motor-preparatory responses are operationalized, and the behavioral or movement controls are not available.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* No content-bearing account of the observations, latency alignment, covariates, or component-identification procedure is present.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The scientific implications of its findings cannot be determined from its title.

## Temporal origin of choice-predictive population activity — Spatial-organization variant

Rejected: the question restates work already published. The prior below does what this version proposed to do.

**Why.** A content-bearing, DOI-identified prior directly maps stimulus-dependent and endogenous choice-predictive components across cortex during perceptual decision-making and contrasts their regional-temporal organization. Its findings already bear on whether sensory-linked and later decision-linked signals are spatially segregated versus broadly distributed, although the candidate's explicit movement-alternative robustness criterion is not established by the cited abstract.

**Compared against 2 records:**

- [10.1038/s41467-020-18826-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18826-6)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both address the cortical distribution of sensory/stimulus-linked and choice-predictive activity during perceptual decisions.
  - *Same tension.* Both contrast a feedforward-style separation of sensory evidence and downstream choice-related activity with broader, potentially feedback-mediated cortical distribution of choice information.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior exhaustively maps stimulus- and choice-specific signals across the human cortical surface in a visual decision task; the candidate asks whether sensory-linked and later decision-linked choice-predictive components occupy distinct processing populations or are distributed across major regions.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior explicitly separates stimulus-dependent from endogenous choice-predictive activity regionally and temporally, finding an endogenous early-visual component that follows the buildup of choice-predictive activity in premotor regions. This directly tests whether component identity and timing have distinct or widespread regional profiles.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* Its observation of choice-predictive activity in early visual cortex together with delayed relationships to premotor buildup supports a distributed, feedback-compatible account rather than a simple confinement of choice information to downstream action regions. The abstract does not establish the candidate's proposed movement-control robustness test or its instability/null criterion.
- [10.1101/2021.03.02.433657](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.03.02.433657)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern whether choice-related information is localized to particular regions or broadly represented across posterior cortical areas.
  - *Same tension.* Both address whether apparent widespread choice activity reflects a distributed decision variable versus signals separable from concurrent sensory and motor-related activity.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior studies mesoscale mouse posterior cortex during visual discrimination, whereas the candidate is stated across major brain regions without fixing species or modality. Both compare distributed choice information with sensory- and motor-related components.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior reports broadly distributed choice signals forming a low-dimensional decision variable and a subspace near-orthogonal to concurrent sensory and motor-related activations. This is directly relevant to the candidate's distinction between distributed activity and movement/common-input alternatives, though it does not test the candidate's proposed regional sensory-versus-later-component profile criterion.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* Broad, sensory/motor-separable choice signals favor a distributed account. The abstract does not report a null or unstable regional-profile outcome and therefore does not support the candidate's full outcome framework.

## Functional geometry of shared neural variability — Sensory-alignment variant

Rejected: the question restates work already published. The prior below does what this version proposed to do.

**Why.** A content-bearing DOI-identified prior directly operationalizes and applies the proposed sensory-evidence/noise-covariance alignment test to identify differential (information-limiting) correlations in macaque V1, including an interpretation in terms of orientation-discrimination thresholds. Other content-bearing priors independently establish that small aggregate pairwise correlations can nevertheless limit information and that sensory-population information can be decomposed into information-limiting versus nonlimiting correlation components. Thus the candidate's general question and its proposed positive implication are already directly addressed within the bounded evidence pack.

**Compared against 3 records:**

- [10.1101/842724](https://doi.org/10.1101/842724)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Direct overlap: the prior investigates whether population noise covariance contains differential, information-limiting variability aligned with the neural sensory-encoding direction.
  - *Same tension.* Direct overlap: it treats correlation magnitude as insufficient for assessing coding limits and identifies covariance orientation relative to the stimulus-encoding direction as the relevant structure.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior studies orientation encoding in macaque V1 and contrasts covariance modes aligned with the encoding direction against modes that do not establish differential correlations. The candidate is population-unspecified, but asks the same general sensory-evidence alignment question.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* Direct overlap: the prior computes alignment of the stimulus encoding direction f′ with eigenvectors of the noise covariance matrix and treats concentration of f′ in high-variance eigenmodes as evidence of strong differential correlations.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior interprets detected alignment as signatures of information-limiting correlations and estimates orientation-discrimination thresholds consistent with behavioral performance. This directly supplies the candidate's positive-result meaning; failure to detect such alignment would correspondingly undermine that interpretation.
- [10.1523/jneurosci.2072-19.2019](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.2072-19.2019)
  - *Same phenomenon.* The prior examines information-limiting noise correlations in large macaque neural populations.
  - *Same tension.* It directly supports the candidate's distinction between aggregate correlation magnitude and information-limiting structure: average pairwise noise correlations were very small while information scaling was strongly sublinear.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior concerns saccade information in prefrontal cortex rather than sensory-evidence directions in an unspecified sensory population, so its empirical target differs.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* Its discriminating observation is sublinear information scaling that becomes linear after trial shuffling destroys correlations, rather than direct covariance alignment with sensory-evidence directions.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* It demonstrates that weak aggregate correlations do not imply negligible coding consequences, reinforcing the candidate's proposed interpretation but not independently establishing the candidate's specific alignment criterion.
- [10.1038/s41467-020-20722-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20722-y)
  - *Same phenomenon.* The prior studies correlated noise and information-limiting versus nonlimiting components in mouse V1 populations encoding visual-motion direction.
  - *Same tension.* It shares the core premise that the coding consequence of correlated variability depends on its information-limiting component, not merely on the existence or average magnitude of correlated noise.
  - *Same contrast.* It is a sensory cortical population and visual-stimulus-direction setting, close in scope to the candidate's generic sensory-evidence framing, though it is specifically mouse V1 and motion direction.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior compartmentalizes noise correlations into information-limiting and nonlimiting components and uses information scaling/extrapolation, rather than the candidate's stated independent sensory-evidence alignment and magnitude-matched comparison.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* Its sublinear scaling and inferred redundant code provide an information-limitation interpretation for correlated variability, consistent with the candidate's proposed positive outcome meaning.

## Functional forms of mixed selectivity in decision populations — Representational-taxonomy variant

Deferred: the search could not retrieve enough to judge the question either way. The records below were read and none of them tests what this version proposed.

**Why.** Content-bearing records establish that nonlinear mixed selectivity, its contrast with additive mixing, and cross-area variation in nonlinear selectivity are established topics. However, none of those records directly tests the candidate's joint two-axis question: conditional sensory-evidence-by-choice or sensory-evidence-by-movement interactions after conditioning on correlated movement, choice, and task-state variables, independently assessed against reproducible discrete-versus-continuous profile organization using a common variable set. A resolved scholarly lead has a title that appears highly germane to the categorical-versus-category-free component, but it is title-only and therefore cannot establish whether it directly answers the candidate question or requires revision. The bounded packet consequently does not support either a direct-recap or close-prior verdict.

**Compared against 3 records:**

- [10.1523/eneuro.0517-21.2022](https://doi.org/10.1523/eneuro.0517-21.2022)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern nonlinear mixed selectivity and comparison of its prevalence or magnitude across major brain populations.
  - *Same tension.* The prior treats nonlinear mixed selectivity as conjunctive task-variable coding and reports area specialization; the candidate additionally distinguishes genuine conditional non-additivity from interaction-like effects induced by correlated movement, choice, and task-state variables.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior contrasts dlPFC with PPC in macaques during a working-memory task. The candidate proposes comparisons across major populations using a shared sensory-evidence, choice, and movement variable set.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior reports differences in NMS frequency and magnitude across areas and session time, but the abstract does not describe conditioning on measured movement and other correlated variables, additive conditional-model comparison, or profile-boundary reproducibility tests.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* Its positive result supports regional specialization in NMS. It does not establish the candidate's proposed separation between conditional interaction structure and discrete-versus-continuous response-profile organization, nor the stated interpretations of null or instability outcomes.
- [10.1016/j.neuron.2024.04.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.04.017)
  - *Same phenomenon.* The prior and candidate both centrally distinguish linear/additive from nonlinear mixed selectivity in neural responses to multiple variables.
  - *Same tension.* The prior defines nonlinear mixed selectivity as activity not expressible as a weighted sum and addresses circuit mechanisms for such mixing. The candidate's added tension is whether apparent interactions survive conditioning on correlated behavioral and state variables, and whether this property is independent of profile-distribution categories.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior is a broad mechanistic account of neural circuits and variable mixtures rather than a cross-population comparison under the candidate's specified sensory-evidence, choice, and movement variables.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior supplies a conceptual additive-versus-nonlinear criterion, but does not provide the candidate's required conditional interaction test, movement-controlled comparison, resampling-based profile organization analysis, or common-variable population contrast.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior links diverse nonlinear mixed selectivity to high-dimensional representations and flexible readout. It does not address how positive, negative, or unstable results should jointly distinguish covariation, conditional non-additivity, and discrete versus continuum organization.
- [10.1016/j.conb.2022.102644](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2022.102644)
  - *Same phenomenon.* The title directly signals overlap with the candidate's categorical-versus-category-free mixed-selectivity organization question.
  - *Same tension.* The title suggests relevance to the relation between categorical and category-free mixed selectivity and representational geometry, but provides no content from which to determine whether it treats this relation as independent of nonlinear/additive interaction form or addresses correlated behavioral covariates.
  - *Same contrast.* The title does not identify populations, task variables, or the cross-population comparison scope needed to assess overlap with the candidate.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* No abstract or excerpt is available. The record cannot establish whether the prior uses reproducible profile boundaries, resampling, sensitivity analysis, conditional interaction estimates, or an additive conditional-response comparison.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* No content-bearing evidence is available to determine whether the prior assigns the same scientific meaning to positive, negative, or unstable findings.

## Population geometry of brain-wide sensorimotor transformation — Geometry-invariance variant

Deferred: published work sits close enough that the question would have to be made distinguishable from it first. Not the same question, and not a free one.

**Why.** No content-bearing prior in the bounded pack directly tests preservation versus ordered reconfiguration of sensory-evidence/choice relational geometry across the proposed visual-sensory, association/frontomotor, and premotor sequence. However, a resolved scholarly prior already compares evidence- and choice-related population dynamics in PMd and M1 and reports an earlier/stronger PMd decision variable under one timing regime, with the areas becoming largely indistinguishable under temporal uncertainty. The candidate must make its geometric, multi-stage, and timing-regime distinction scientifically testable rather than allowing a result based on regional decision-variable onset or strength to be interpreted as ordered representational transformation.

**Compared against 2 records:**

- [10.1101/283960](https://doi.org/10.1101/283960)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both address how sensory evidence and eventual choice are represented in populations spanning premotor and motor circuits before an arm-movement report.
  - *Same tension.* The prior contrasts regional timing/strength and stable recruitment accounts of decision-variable representation; the proposal contrasts stable shared relational geometry with a staged representational transformation. The prior therefore bears on, but does not resolve, the proposal's transformation interpretation.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior directly compares PMd with M1 during a random-dots perceptual decision with arm movements. The proposal includes the comparable premotor/motor portion but additionally requires a visual-sensory to association/frontomotor to premotor sequence and compares relational geometry rather than only decision-variable dynamics.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior finds that PMd decision variables are earlier and stronger than M1 under fixed predictable stimulus duration, but that the two become largely indistinguishable under temporal uncertainty; it also reports stable within-epoch choice-related population activity after recruitment. This is not a test of rotation/reflection/scale-invariant cross-region geometry or a monotonic multi-region geometric reconfiguration.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior shows that apparent PMd-to-M1 differences can depend on stimulus temporal regime and need not reflect trivially faster motor kinematics. Thus a regional lead or stronger choice signal alone cannot support the proposed ordered-geometry account; convergence of PMd and M1 under uncertainty would also not by itself establish shared geometry without the specified relational comparison.
- [10.1101/2021.01.04.425244](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.01.04.425244)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern the geometry relating sensory evidence, decision variables, and action-related representations in neural populations.
  - *Same tension.* The prior challenges a context-invariant action-plan decision-variable account by reporting task-context-dependent rotations of decision manifolds and distinct readouts. The proposal instead asks whether geometry is preserved or changes in an ordered regional sequence during a single evidence-to-choice process.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior concerns LIP and lateral/medial prefrontal decision circuits across face and motion task contexts, not a visual-sensory through premotor/motor-output sequence or a pre-movement cross-region progression.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior's key observation is curved population manifolds whose orientation rotates with task context and is misaligned with action representations. It does not test whether a common set of sensory-evidence-by-choice condition relations is preserved, up to specified transformations, across regions and epochs.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior establishes that geometric changes can reflect task-context-specific decision formation and readout, rather than an ordered sensorimotor transformation. Accordingly, any geometry difference in the proposed contrast requires interpretation relative to matched task context and common condition structure; a rotation alone is not evidence of a regional transformation sequence.
