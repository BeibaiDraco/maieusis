## Question the system extracted

What is the effect of changes in the Southern Hemisphere zonal-mean wind on eddies, and how do zonal-wind/eddy interactions account for the persistence, low-frequency variance, poleward propagation, and selection of the dominant mode of zonal-mean variability?

## The scientific contrast

Whether eddy forcing is primarily stochastic forcing of the zonal wind or is partly organized by zonal-mean-wind anomalies through a positive feedback, and whether the feedback that sustains the leading EOF differs from the feedback associated with poleward propagation and from the behavior of EOF2.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- The anomalous eddy forcing is essentially stochastic and independent of the zonal-mean wind.
- A positive zonal-wind/eddy feedback organizes part of the low-frequency eddy forcing, reinforces EOF1 anomalies, and increases their persistence and variance.
- A propagating feedback, rather than a sustaining positive feedback, explains poleward movement of zonal-wind anomalies.
- Residual low-frequency barotropic eddies oppose the synoptic positive feedback and damp the jets created by baroclinic eddies.
- EOF1 may be more persistent and dominant because of positive feedback, while differences in random eddy-forcing strength also contribute to its greater variance than EOF2.

What would tell them apart: Lagged momentum-budget diagnostics and time-filtered eddy statistics distinguish wind-forced eddy responses from stochastic forcing: a positive feedback should appear as consistently positive eddy-forcing correlations at lags greater than a week, should be stronger for EOF1 than EOF2, and should project onto EOF1; the propagation mechanism should appear as eddy forcing in quadrature with the wind anomalies and as poleward drift.

## Background and motivation

- The dominant Southern Hemisphere zonal-mean wind variability is a robust equivalent-barotropic dipole representing north–south fluctuations of the midlatitude jet around its mean position near 50°S, with substantial variance on timescales longer than one month.
- Simple atmospheric models with time-independent external forcing can produce an index cycle, indicating that a substantial portion of Southern Hemisphere midlatitude variability may be unforced variability arising from internal atmospheric dynamics.
- Eddies drive changes in the zonal-mean wind, but it remained controversial whether changes in the zonal-mean flow substantially affect eddy momentum fluxes, or equivalently whether an important zonal-mean-wind/eddy feedback exists.
- Several observational studies suggested that zonal-mean-wind/eddy feedback increases zonal-index persistence and low-frequency variance, whereas Feldstein and Lee questioned the importance of positive feedback from total eddy forcing and found that low- and cross-frequency contributions could prevent the eddies from increasing zonal-index persistence.
- Eddy momentum fluxes had also been found to cause poleward propagation of zonal-mean wind anomalies, raising the possibility that persistence and propagation reflect different aspects of eddy–zonal-flow interaction.
- The relatively small seasonal cycle of Southern Hemisphere midlatitude oceanic conditions makes internal atmospheric dynamical processes an important focus for explaining zonal-mean variability.

## Why this question mattered

Determining whether and how eddies feed back on zonal-mean wind explains why the leading Southern Hemisphere mode is unusually persistent and dominant, separates persistence from poleward propagation, and identifies dynamical processes responsible for a major component of low-frequency atmospheric variability.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Observational analysis of Southern Hemisphere zonal-mean zonal-wind variability and its interaction with eddy momentum forcing over 1978–1997, using EOF analysis, time filtering, spectral and cross-spectral analysis, composites, lagged regressions, momentum-budget diagnostics, and a simple linear feedback model.

- Population: Southern Hemisphere atmosphere, represented on a 2.5° × 2.5° latitude/longitude grid.
- Measurements: Wind and temperature at 12 pressure levels from 1000 to 100 mb, Zonal-mean zonal wind anomalies, EOF spatial patterns and principal components, Eddy momentum-flux convergence and forcing, Eddy temperature fluxes and EP flux vectors, Power spectra, autocorrelation, cross-correlation, and coherence, Meridional temperature-gradient-based baroclinicity, External Rossby-wave index of refraction
