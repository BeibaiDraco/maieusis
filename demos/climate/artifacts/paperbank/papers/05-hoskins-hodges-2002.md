## Question the system extracted

How do Eulerian filtered-variance and system-centered feature-tracking diagnostics, applied across a wide range of meteorological fields and atmospheric levels, characterize the Northern Hemisphere winter storm tracks, and what new insight do their combined perspectives provide about storm-track organization and possible mechanisms?

## The scientific contrast

Grid-point, bandpass-filtered variance diagnostics versus objective feature tracking of synoptic systems, compared across fields from mean sea level pressure and vorticity to upper-tropospheric potential-vorticity-related variables.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Storm-track patterns may reflect broad variance in fields without corresponding to coherent individual systems.
- Differences between diagnostics may arise from the meteorological field used, including large-scale background masking, field smoothness, spatial scale, or sensitivity to extrapolation and topography.
- Upper-tropospheric differences may reflect the reanalysis or data-assimilation system rather than atmospheric storm-track structure alone.
- An upper-tropospheric spiral or waveguide-like band may provide perturbations that amplify through the depth where lower-tropospheric conditions are favorable, rather than lower-tropospheric tracks being a single continuous population of systems.

What would tell them apart: Comparing filtered variances with feature, track, genesis, lysis, intensity, and trajectory diagnostics across multiple fields and levels can show whether apparent storm-track bands correspond to coherent systems, whether cyclonic and anticyclonic activity differ, and whether upper-level features connect spatially with lower-level genesis and amplification regions.

## Background and motivation

- Storm tracks have traditionally been diagnosed either by tracking weather systems and summarizing their trajectories or by calculating statistics such as band-limited variance at grid points.
- Eulerian methods provide a general storm-track activity measure but do not fully identify the types and attributes of systems constituting that activity.
- Earlier feature-tracking studies generally used a restricted set of fields such as mean sea level pressure, 500-hPa geopotential, or lower-tropospheric vorticity, even though these fields have different limitations for identifying synoptic systems.
- Different meteorological fields produce broadly similar storm-track patterns but also significant differences in the locations, magnitudes, and vertical structures of storm-track activity.
- Upper-tropospheric tracking results can be sensitive to the data-assimilation and analysis system, with more coherent small-scale structures in some NCEP, ECMWF operational, and GEOS-1 products than in ERA-15.

## Why this question mattered

The combined diagnostics can distinguish general activity measures from the behavior of the systems that generate them, reveal cyclonic and anticyclonic differences, identify genesis and lysis pathways, and provide evidence about how upper-tropospheric disturbances may relate to lower-tropospheric storm development.
