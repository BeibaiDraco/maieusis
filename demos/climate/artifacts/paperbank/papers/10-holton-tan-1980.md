## Question the system extracted

Does the equatorial QBO modulate the extratropical and global atmospheric circulation at 50 mb, including zonal-mean circulation and stationary planetary-wave components, and does the response depend on season and hemisphere?

## The scientific contrast

Atmospheric circulation during westerly versus easterly phases of the equatorial QBO, especially across winter and summer conditions and between the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- The apparent extratropical QBO could reflect ordinary interannual variability in the general circulation rather than a direct relationship with the equatorial QBO.
- The late-winter planetary-wavenumber-2 signal could be a sampling fluctuation, particularly because its support weakened when an additional four-year sample was added.
- Differences in planetary-wave amplitudes and phases could arise from the latitude shift of the critical line, from changes in the broader mean-zonal-wind distribution, or from both.
- Some of the 50 mb geopotential-height signal could be a barotropic response to atmospheric-mass changes between polar and middle latitudes rather than a change in 50–1000 mb thickness.

What would tell them apart: Comparing QBO-phase composites across seasons, hemispheres, zonal-mean fields, and planetary-wave components can distinguish a reproducible QBO-related signal from sampling variability and can test whether wave-related responses occur preferentially in seasons with active planetary waves.

## Background and motivation

- The equatorial QBO is an approximately 20-month oscillation characterized by alternating downward-propagating westerly and easterly mean zonal winds in the equatorial lower stratosphere.
- The equatorial QBO is generally believed to be a nonlinear oscillation produced by vertical momentum transfer by equatorial waves.
- Earlier studies reported or sought extratropical QBOs, but many used quite short data records.
- The irregular period of the equatorial QBO and the relatively short observational record made it difficult to determine whether extratropical QBO signals were directly related to the equatorial QBO or merely reflected interannual variability in the general circulation.
- Ebdon’s composites indicated lower polar surface pressure during the westerly equatorial-QBO phase in January, while a limited six-year sample suggested a similar 30 mb signal, but those upper-level results were not displayed.
- The equatorial QBO controls the latitude of the zero-mean-zonal-wind critical line in the lower stratosphere, and planetary-wave structure and amplitude may depend on the location of that critical surface.
- The Southern Hemisphere extratropical QBO reported by Tucker could not be separated into zonal-mean and planetary-wave components because the stations were concentrated along approximately one longitude.

## Why this question mattered

Establishing whether the equatorial QBO modulates global 50 mb circulation would clarify the connection between tropical and extratropical atmospheric variability and constrain explanations involving critical-line shifts, mean-flow changes, and wave–mean-flow interaction. The seasonal comparison also tests when planetary waves contribute to the extratropical response.
