## The scientific contrast

Atmospheric circulation and planetary-wave fields during the westerly phase versus the easterly phase of the equatorial QBO, compared across seasons and hemispheres.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- The observed extratropical signal may be directly related to the equatorial QBO.
- The apparent extratropical QBO may instead reflect interannual variability in the general circulation.
- Differences in planetary-wave amplitude and phase may result from the QBO-associated latitudinal shift of the critical line, from differences in the mean zonal-wind distributions, or from both.
- The wavenumber-2 signal may be a sampling fluctuation, especially given large year-to-year variations and its weaker support in the extended sample.

What would tell them apart: Compare westerly and easterly QBO composites across seasons, hemispheres, zonal-mean and planetary-wave components, and additional years; seasonal consistency and statistically distinguishable phase differences would support a QBO-related circulation signal, whereas weak or unstable signals would favor sampling variability or general interannual variability.

## Background and motivation

- The equatorial QBO is characterized by propagating patterns of westerly and easterly mean zonal winds with an approximately 26-month period.
- Stationary planetary waves can be influenced by the latitude of the critical surface where the mean zonal wind vanishes, and this critical surface depends on season and height.
- Because planetary waves interact with the stratospheric mean flow, the extratropical response to the equatorial QBO cannot be predicted in a simple fashion from the available theoretical considerations.
- Previous studies had attempted to identify QBO-like signals at extratropical latitudes, but relatively short data records made it difficult to determine whether those signals were directly related to the equatorial QBO or merely reflected interannual variability in the general circulation.
- The phase of the equatorial QBO is associated with differences in Northern Hemisphere 50-mb geopotential heights, zonal-mean circulation, and planetary-wave behavior, but the strength and form of these differences vary by season and wavenumber.
- The Southern Hemisphere data show a rapid decrease in QBO amplitude away from the equator, with the reported signal differing between spring and summer composites.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question would determine whether extratropical circulation anomalies identified in earlier short-record studies are related to the equatorial QBO, establish whether the response varies seasonally and by planetary wavenumber, and constrain interpretation of the mechanisms involving the critical line and the extratropical mean zonal wind.
