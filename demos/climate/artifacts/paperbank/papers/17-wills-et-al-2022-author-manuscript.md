## Question the system extracted

Can internal multi-decadal variability as represented in 16 initial-condition climate-model large ensembles explain the mismatch between observed and modeled SST and SLP trends over 1979–2020, or does the mismatch indicate biases in the modeled transient response to external forcing?

## The scientific contrast

Anomalous internal variability versus systematic climate-model bias in the forced response to historical anthropogenic forcing.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Anomalous multi-decadal internal atmosphere–ocean variability could produce the observed Pacific and Southern Ocean trends.
- Systematic biases in the transient response to external forcing could cause models to miss the observed trends.
- Model biases in the amplitude or pattern of multi-decadal variability could also contribute to the discrepancy.

What would tell them apart: If the observed trends lie outside the distributions generated by internal variability in the large ensembles, particularly in regions and indices with weak modeled multi-decadal variability, that supports a contribution from biases in the forced response rather than internal variability alone.

## Background and motivation

- Regional climate change depends not only on the magnitude of global warming but also on the spatial pattern of warming.
- The Walker circulation is generally projected by climate models to weaken under anthropogenic greenhouse-gas forcing, with enhanced warming in the eastern equatorial Pacific.
- Observations show enhanced warming in the Indo-Pacific Warm Pool, weak cooling in the eastern equatorial Pacific, and a strengthening of the tropical Pacific east–west SST gradient over recent decades.
- Sea-level-pressure observations indicate Walker-circulation strengthening since 1979, in contrast to climate-model hindcasts, while the Southern Ocean has also shown cooling and sea-ice expansion unlike the anthropogenically forced changes in climate models.
- Observed SST and SLP trends over 1979–2020 are highly anomalous in several regions and indices compared with the combination of forced response and internal variability simulated by the multi-model large ensemble.
- The relative Indo-Pacific Warm Pool warming rate is particularly discrepant between observations and models, while models show little multi-decadal variability in Warm Pool SST.

## Why this question mattered

Distinguishing internal variability from model bias is important for interpreting recent climate trends and for improving confidence in future SST patterns, atmospheric circulation, regional climate impacts, and multi-decadal projections. The paper states that without understanding the anomalous trends or when delayed-warming regions will warm, a major source of uncertainty remains in regional and global climate projections.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Comparison of observed 1979–2020 annual-mean SST and SLP trends with simulations from 16 initial-condition climate-model large ensembles, separating the forced response represented by ensemble means from internal variability represented by ensemble spread; the study also applies signal-to-noise-maximizing pattern analysis.

- Population: Global climate system, with emphasis on the Indo-Pacific, tropical Pacific, Southern Ocean, North Pacific, North Atlantic, and related atmospheric circulation patterns.
- Measurements: annual-mean SST trends, annual-mean SLP trends, Pacific SST gradient, Walker circulation strength, Southern Ocean SST, Indo-Pacific Warm Pool warming relative to tropical-mean warming, signal-to-noise-maximizing SST/SLP patterns
