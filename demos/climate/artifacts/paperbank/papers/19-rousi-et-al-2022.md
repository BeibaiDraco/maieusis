## Question the system extracted

How are European temperature extremes linked to large-scale atmospheric circulation, particularly jet-stream states, and how might changes in those states have contributed to upward heatwave trends? More specifically, is the accelerated western European heatwave trend linked to increasing persistence of double jets in the upper troposphere?

## The scientific contrast

Whether the accelerated European heatwave trend is explained only by direct thermodynamic warming and local feedbacks, or whether changing atmospheric dynamics—especially more frequent and persistent Eurasian double-jet states—make a distinct contribution.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Direct thermodynamic warming from increasing greenhouse-gas concentrations can increase heatwave intensity and frequency.
- Local soil-moisture and land-atmosphere feedbacks may amplify heatwaves, particularly in Mediterranean and eastern European regions.
- Oceanic circulation, sea-surface temperatures, AMOC-related responses, tropical Rossby-wave forcing, aerosol forcing, or internal atmospheric dynamics may alter European summer circulation.
- Blocking anticyclones may be caused by double-jet configurations, but the reverse causal chain is also possible: blocking may split the jet into two branches and create a double-jet state.
- Double jets and blocking may instead be two aspects of a shared dynamical flow pattern rather than a simple one-way causal chain.

What would tell them apart: A distinct dynamical contribution would be supported if double-jet frequency or persistence covaries with European heatwave metrics and its upward trend reproduces a substantial portion of the European trend after accounting, as a first-order approximation, for the common midlatitude thermodynamic trend.

## Background and motivation

- European heat extremes have increased particularly strongly since the 2003 heatwave, and European heatwaves are projected to increase disproportionately relative to the global mean temperature, while the underlying reasons remain poorly understood.
- European summer heat extremes and their variability are influenced by large-scale atmospheric circulation and jet-stream states, soil-moisture and land-atmosphere feedbacks, and oceanic circulation and sea-surface temperatures.
- Blocking anticyclones are associated with northern-midlatitude summer heat extremes, and blocking is often linked to a double jet-stream structure over Eurasia; however, Rossby-wave breaking and blocking may also cause the jet to split into a double-jet state.
- Anthropogenic warming can increase heatwave intensity and frequency through direct warming and can also alter the drivers of natural variability.
- High-latitude land warming and Arctic amplification may favor more frequent or persistent double-jet states by strengthening the Arctic-front jet and altering the relevant thermal gradients.

## Why this question mattered

The question separates dynamical changes from direct thermodynamic warming and local feedbacks in explaining why European heatwaves are increasing faster than in other northern-midlatitude regions. Resolving this contribution can improve understanding of European heatwave mechanisms, help diagnose why climate models may underestimate western European warming, and inform risk assessment for persistent and potentially concurrent extremes.
