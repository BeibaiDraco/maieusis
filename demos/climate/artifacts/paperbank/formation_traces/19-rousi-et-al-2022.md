# Question-formation trace

- Review status: `ai_reviewed`

## Starting background

- European heat extremes have increased strongly, while their disproportionate regional trend is not well understood.
- Large-scale circulation and jet-stream states are among the processes associated with European summer heat extremes, alongside thermodynamic warming and land–atmosphere or oceanic influences.
- Blocking and double-jet configurations are closely related atmospheric states, but the direction and nature of their relationship remain uncertain.

## Unresolved gap

The increasing trend in European heatwaves is not accompanied by clear evidence of corresponding changes in the frequency or intensity of European summer blocking. The provided evidence therefore frames an unresolved possibility that changes in the frequency or persistence of double-jet states, rather than blocking frequency alone, contribute to the accelerated western European heatwave trend; the observational relationship does not by itself establish whether double jets cause blocking, result from blocking, or reflect a shared flow regime.

## Dataset opportunity

ERA5 supplies daily, spatially resolved Eurasian upper-tropospheric wind fields together with European surface-temperature fields across 1979–2020, allowing circulation states, their persistence, and regional heatwave metrics to be represented in a common longitudinal record.

Because the same daily record contains circulation and temperature information over the relevant Eurasian and European domains, daily jet configurations can be grouped into recurring states and their frequency or persistence can be compared with heatwave frequency, cumulative intensity, spatial signatures, and trends. This makes it possible to test whether a dynamical signal contributes beyond a broad thermodynamic warming background, while retaining the distinction between association and causal attribution.

## Resulting question

Do changes in the frequency or persistence of Eurasian double-jet states help explain the accelerated increase in western European heatwave frequency and cumulative intensity, and how does this dynamical association compare with explanations based on direct thermodynamic warming and local feedbacks?

## Scientific consequence

If more-persistent double-jet states covary with and account for a substantial part of the enhanced western European heatwave trend, the regional acceleration would involve a distinct change in atmospheric dynamics rather than direct warming alone. If the relationship is weak or absent, the discrepancy would instead point toward thermodynamic, land-surface, oceanic, or other circulation mechanisms. Either outcome would also constrain interpretations of the relationship between double jets and blocking, although the observational design cannot by itself determine causal direction.

The question is scientifically valuable because it turns a regional climate trend with an unresolved dynamical explanation into a mechanistic comparison between circulation regimes and temperature extremes. It links atmospheric-state variability to spatially resolved impacts, potentially informing evaluation of climate-model circulation variability and interpretation of risks from persistent or concurrent heat extremes. As a PaperBank pattern, it illustrates how an existing reanalysis can be reorganized around physically meaningful states to distinguish a regional dynamical contribution from a broad background trend.
