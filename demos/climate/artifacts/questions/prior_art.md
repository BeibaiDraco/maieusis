# What prior-art review found, and which questions it stopped

Before a question may be planned it is checked against the published record. This page is every version that check did not let through, with the work it ran into.

A stop here is not a verdict on the dataset and not a fault. It is the review doing its job: the questions that got through are the ones that survived it, and reading this page is how you tell that claim from an assertion.

Every record named below resolves to a DOI. A model's impression that something exists is not enough to stop a question; the prior has to resolve to an identity a reader can open. The prose under each one is the reviewer's own account of the overlap, not a summary written afterwards.

## Lifecycle asymmetry and dynamical memory in vortex disturbances — History-conditioned recovery

Deferred: published work sits close enough that the question would have to be made distinguishable from it first. Not the same question, and not a free one.

**Why.** The pack does not contain a prior that directly tests whether a temporally isolated, pre-weakening planetary-wave forcing history adds conditional predictive information for lower-stratospheric recovery persistence after matching the first weak-vortex state and controlling subsequent wave forcing. However, content-bearing scholarly priors show that extended lower-stratospheric recovery is already mechanistically tied to suppressed post-warming wave driving and weak radiative cooling, and that precursor wave activity and morphology are associated with subsequent SSW evolution. The proposed question therefore needs a sharper scientific separation between antecedent-history information and (i) recovery-period wave driving, (ii) radiative relaxation and contemporaneous state, and (iii) morphology/regime-linked pathways, so that its claimed dynamical-memory delta is testable rather than a relabeling of known recovery distinctions.

**Compared against 3 records:**

- [10.1175/jas-d-12-0111.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jas-d-12-0111.1)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both address persistence of anomalous lower-stratospheric conditions during recovery after SSWs, particularly extended polar-vortex recoveries.
  - *Same tension.* The prior attributes persistent lower-stratospheric anomalies to strongly suppressed wave driving and weak radiative cooling, whereas the candidate asks whether antecedent planetary-wave histories retain information beyond the matched reference-stage state and recovery-period wave forcing.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior contrasts PJO/extended-recovery events with SSWs generally; the candidate contrasts pre-weakening forcing-history classes among episodes matched on weak-vortex state, season, residual circulation, and thermal structure and purports to exclude morphology/recovery-regime labels.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior's discriminating evidence is persistent lower-stratospheric warmth together with suppressed wave driving and weak radiative cooling. The candidate's proposed discriminator is a conditional difference in later recovery persistence by pre-weakening forcing-history class after accounting for recovery-period forcing and contemporaneous conditions.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior establishes that post-warming forcing and radiative response can explain extended recovery. A candidate positive result would only support added antecedent information if it remains distinguishable from those established contemporaneous recovery controls; a null result would specifically weigh against added history information under the stated conditioning, not against wave-driven or radiative recovery dynamics generally.
- [10.1175/jcli-d-18-0053.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jcli-d-18-0053.1)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern planetary-wave activity before SSW onset and consequences following SSWs, including distinctions in downward influence and morphology.
  - *Same tension.* The prior shows that precursor wave activity is probabilistically associated with later downward versus non-downward propagation and that split and displacement events differ in their evolution. The candidate proposes a stronger memory claim: antecedent wave histories add information after state matching, post-reference forcing control, and exclusion of morphology/regime labels.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior contrasts downward and non-downward propagating SSWs and examines split/displacement events; the candidate targets lower-stratospheric recovery persistence and seeks forcing-history classes independent of established morphology or recovery-regime labels.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior uses differing precursor wave activity and later downward/tropospheric influence, with morphology-linked evolution. The candidate uses conditional lower-stratospheric recovery persistence by classes defined solely in a fixed pre-weakening planetary-wave window.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior supports the premise that antecedent wave activity can be predictively informative, but it does not establish conditional information beyond matched vortex state and subsequent forcing, nor does it use lower-stratospheric recovery persistence as the target. A candidate positive result must therefore demonstrate information not attributable to precursor associations with morphology or a broader downward-propagation pathway; a null would not contradict the prior's unconditional/probabilistic precursor associations.
- [10.1029/2009gl038586](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009gl038586)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern differences in lower-stratospheric vortex recovery after major Arctic SSWs and differences between split and displacement events.
  - *Same tension.* The prior reports that many differences between the 2006 displacement and 2009 split events appear related to differing SSW character. The candidate posits that antecedent planetary-wave histories may add information apart from morphology labels and contemporaneous matched state.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior is a two-event observational comparison with morphology confounded with year and event character. The candidate proposes a cross-episode conditional contrast with explicit matching and exclusions.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior observes slower reversion to westerlies and no significant lower-stratospheric vortex recovery in 2009 versus 2006. The candidate would require a reproducible persistence difference between pre-weakening forcing-history classes after controls, not merely an event-to-event difference.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior demonstrates that lower-stratospheric recovery can vary strongly across SSW events and coincide with morphology-related character. It does not show that pre-weakening forcing history independently predicts that variation. A positive candidate result must remain after separating the history classes from event-character and morphology-linked differences; a null would not negate the observed event contrast.

## Regime dependence in wave–mean-flow organization — Vertical-expression regime comparison

Deferred: published work sits close enough that the question would have to be made distinguishable from it first. Not the same question, and not a free one.

**Why.** Content-bearing DOI records establish that split and displacement SSWs already differ systematically in vertical simultaneity/barotropicity, preconditioning and persistence. The candidate does not directly recap a prior because it proposes a thresholded 100–10-hPa coherence contrast, conditioned on local magnitude and morphology, and tests both antecedent forcing and post-peak recovery. However, the proposed coherent/confined classification risks being an alternative encoding of the established split–displacement vertical-structure distinction unless it is shown to retain variation and explanatory meaning within morphology classes.

**Compared against 3 records:**

- [10.1175/2008jcli2365.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/2008jcli2365.1)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern the vertical evolution of the polar vortex during major SSWs.
  - *Same tension.* The prior distinguishes barotropic, near-simultaneous splitting from baroclinic, vertically tilted displacement evolution; this directly bears on whether coordinated cross-level transition is an independent regime rather than established vortex geometry.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior contrasts split versus displacement SSWs, whereas the candidate contrasts coherent versus confined transitions while proposing to account for morphology and local magnitude.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior reports near-simultaneous vortex splitting across 20–40 km versus vertically separated/tilted displacement structure. This substantially overlaps the candidate's temporal cross-level coordination criterion, though the candidate specifies same-signed threshold crossings over 100–10 hPa and a confined comparator.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior establishes morphology-linked vertical structures but does not test whether coherence, conditional on morphology and local magnitude, predicts antecedent planetary-wave forcing or independently defined recovery.
- [10.1175/jas-d-14-0026.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jas-d-14-0026.1)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both address SSW vortex evolution and antecedent wave-related dynamical forcing/preconditioning.
  - *Same tension.* The prior tests distinct forcing accounts for split and displacement events, including resonance, planetary-wave drag, and gravity-wave drag; the candidate tests whether vertical coherence identifies a distinct integrated wave–mean-flow regime.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior's primary contrast is split versus displacement composites. The candidate's contrast is coherent versus confined events after accounting for split/displacement morphology and other descriptors.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* Both rely on vertical vortex structure and prewarming wave diagnostics. The prior does not define or evaluate the candidate's thresholded, multi-level same-signed coherence measure, nor its matched local-magnitude comparison.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior finds morphology-associated differences in preconditioning and supports a resonance account for splits; it does not establish whether coherence has antecedent-forcing or recovery associations independent of morphology.
- [10.1175/jcli3996.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jcli3996.1)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both examine major midwinter SSW evolution, vortex morphology, and persistence of stratospheric anomalies.
  - *Same tension.* The prior identifies dynamical distinctions and preconditioning between splitting and displacement events; the candidate asks whether cross-level coordination adds dynamical meaning beyond morphology and severity-related descriptors.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior compares split and nonsplit/displacement events. The candidate must compare coherent and confined cases in a way that is not merely a relabeling of that morphology contrast.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior reports longer-lived middle-stratospheric temperature influence for split events and preconditioning differences. The candidate instead requires a prescribed vertical timing pattern and evaluates post-peak recovery conditional on alternatives.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* Existing morphology-associated persistence differences mean a coherence–recovery association alone would not support incremental meaning unless it remains after a scientifically adequate within-morphology or morphology-balanced comparison.
