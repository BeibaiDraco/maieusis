## Family 001: Recurrent states versus transition-centered organization of the polar stratosphere

- Shortlist disposition: **Shortlisted for planning; this is not scientific approval**
- Proposal review status: `model_generated`
- Authority ceiling: `provisional_inspiration`

### Scientific background

Tests whether large-scale stratospheric variability is most informatively organized as recurrent states or as dynamically distinctive transition episodes, without assuming that either representation is uniquely physical.

### Shared scientific tension

Long-lived circulation states may be substantive recurrent organizations of the system, but apparent regimes can also emerge from continuous evolution whose scientifically distinctive structure is concentrated near transitions.

### Family structure

- Semantic axes: target_contrast; discriminating_observation; outcome_meaning
- Distinctions that should not be merged: State recurrence concerns the scientific validity and added meaning of discrete states.; Transition organization concerns whether state-boundary episodes possess dynamics distinct from ordinary within-state evolution.
- Proposal-stage uncertainties: Whether discrete states are physically substantive or representation-dependent; How to distinguish transition dynamics from seasonal drift; Whether the coarse dataset description supports the required joint comparisons
- Dataset assumptions: Vertically resolved repeated observations may permit comparisons of states and transitions.; The seasonal background may support later control of annual-cycle structure.; No exact feasibility, coverage, or variable availability is established.

### Variant 001.001: State-centered representation test

- Shortlist disposition: **Active for planning**
- Distinction axes: target_contrast, outcome_meaning
- Distinct from sibling variants: This variant asks whether recurrent states themselves are robust and informative; its sibling treats transition episodes, rather than state occupancy, as the primary scientific object.

#### Question

Do recurrent vertically organized circulation states recur robustly across seasonal and multi-decade partitions, and do they provide information about persistence beyond a continuous circulation description?

#### Scientific tension and why it matters

A compact state inventory could reveal recurring dynamical organization, or it could discretize a continuous and seasonally evolving circulation in a method-dependent way.

Establishing whether recurrent states have reproducible vertical and temporal organization would clarify when regime language adds scientific meaning beyond continuous indices.

Separates two temporal interpretations of wave–mean-flow interaction: forcing as an antecedent of circulation change and background circulation as a conditioner of the response to forcing.

#### Dataset leverage hypothesis — not a feasibility certification

Time-varying fields, seasonal backgrounds, departures, and multiple dynamical perspectives may allow later planning of state-congruent and residual change comparisons.

#### Competing explanations

- Within-state change is mainly amplification or weakening of established state structure.
- Wave or eddy relationships change within nominally comparable states, producing structured residuals.
- Changing state boundaries create artificial within-state differences.
- Dataset inhomogeneity produces apparent residual change.

#### Discriminating observation

Altered within-state dynamics would be supported if comparable states showed reproducible residual changes across vertical circulation and forcing perspectives after accounting for state-congruent and seasonal structure.

#### What different outcomes would mean

- Positive: Structured residuals would indicate that historical change modifies internal state dynamics and cannot be summarized by occupancy or mode amplitude alone.
- Negative: Predominantly state-congruent change would support a simpler amplitude or redistribution account.
- Null: Residuals lacking recurrence or diagnostic coherence would remain ambiguous between weak physical change and data or representation sensitivity.

#### Ambiguities

- comparable state
- state-congruent change
- structured residual
- historical stability
- internal state dynamics

#### Planning challenges

- Defining a reference state independently of target change
- Avoiding interpretation of residuals as automatically physical
- Comparing derived diagnostics with shared errors
- Assessing temporal consistency of the reanalysis-derived package

#### Dataset assumptions

- The record may permit broad temporal comparisons within recurring circulation contexts.
- Complementary vertical and forcing descriptions may support residual interpretation after inspection.
- No causal attribution or external-driver inference is supported by this dataset alone.
