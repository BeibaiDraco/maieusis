## Family 001: Wave forcing versus vortex susceptibility before rapid weakening

- Shortlist disposition: **Shortlisted for planning; this is not scientific approval**
- Proposal review status: `model_generated`
- Authority ceiling: `verified`

### Scientific background

Tests whether rapid polar-vortex weakening is organized mainly by anomalous wave forcing or by the susceptibility of the pre-existing stratospheric state. The two variants separate event-level precursor discrimination from a state-conditioned response comparison.

### Shared scientific tension

Upward planetary-wave forcing is central to vortex variability, but the literature remains divided over whether unusually strong forcing or a preconditioned, receptive vortex state better distinguishes rapid weakening episodes.

### Family structure

- Semantic axes: theoretical_tension; target_contrast; discriminating_observation; claim_level
- Distinctions that should not be merged: Predicting or discriminating weakening from precursor histories is not the same claim as estimating response heterogeneity under comparable forcing.; The first variant contrasts predictor families across episodes; the second contrasts antecedent populations under a constrained forcing context.
- Proposal-stage uncertainties: Whether forcing and antecedent-state constructs can be defined independently; Whether the narrow high-latitude representation captures scientifically important vertical forcing distinctions; Whether recurrent episodes provide enough contrasting histories; Whether findings would survive alternative event definitions
- Dataset assumptions: The package may permit aligned histories of broad wave, forcing, reference-flow, and circulation quantities.; A later planner must verify units, equations, signs, temporal mapping, and missing-value handling.; No exact feasibility, event count, or joint coverage is assumed.

### Variant 001.001: Event-level precursor discrimination

- Shortlist disposition: **Active for planning**
- Distinction axes: theoretical_tension, discriminating_observation, claim_level
- Distinct from sibling variants: This variant asks which history best discriminates episode onset; it does not ask whether identical forcing has state-dependent consequences.

Do histories of anomalous wave activity and eddy forcing distinguish rapid polar-vortex weakening episodes more consistently than histories of the pre-existing zonal-mean circulation?

#### Scientific tension and why it matters

A forcing-dominant account predicts a distinctive precursor surge, whereas a susceptibility account predicts that similar forcing histories can have different outcomes depending on the antecedent vortex state.

Separating these observational signatures would refine the contested interpretation of stratospheric precursors without claiming causal identification.

#### Discriminating observation

- Negative: If forcing histories do not distinguish outcomes but antecedent circulation does, the scientific emphasis would shift toward state-dependent susceptibility.
- Null: If neither history discriminates reproducibly, rapid weakening may require interacting ingredients, finer event distinctions, or constructs unavailable in the coarse proposal context.
