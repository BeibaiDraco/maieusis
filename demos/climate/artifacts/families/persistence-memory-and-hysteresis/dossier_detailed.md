## Why this question matters

Distinguishes persistence generated by recurrent state occupancy from path dependence in which circulation outcomes depend on prior forcing and transition history.

The scientific tension is:

Low-frequency persistence may arise from state switching without long intrinsic memory, while asymmetric trajectories and recovery may indicate that forcing history carries predictive information beyond the current circulation state.

## Variant 2: Conditional incremental-prediction test of history-dependent recovery within explicitly represented vortex-event categories

### Why it matters

Determining whether antecedent history adds predictive information beyond an explicitly defined current-state and ongoing-forcing baseline would clarify whether lifecycle history is needed to describe recovery, rather than merely reproducing established event-type or wave-behavior contrasts.

**Original Question Scientist proposal**

For similar displaced circulation states, does prior wave-forcing and transition history predict distinct recovery trajectories beyond the information in the current vertical state and seasonal context?

**Post-novelty revised proposal**

Within displacement, split, and completed displacement-to-split categories, does antecedent wave forcing or completed transition history incrementally predict recovery trajectories after conditioning on season, a current-state representation of vertical circulation morphology and recovery stage, and contemporaneous or recovery-period wave–mean-flow conditions?

### Scientific stakes

**Discriminating observation**

The history-dependent account would be supported only if adding antecedent forcing or completed transition history improves out-of-sample prediction of the recovery trajectory beyond a seasonal baseline containing vertical circulation strength and structure, vortex morphology, recovery stage, and contemporaneous or recovery-period wave–mean-flow conditions, with the increment present within explicitly stratified displacement, split, and completed displacement-to-split categories rather than arising solely across them.

**What possible outcomes would mean**

- Positive pattern: A reproducible within-category predictive increment from antecedent history would support history-aware recovery descriptions and indicate that the defined current-state and continuing-forcing representation is not sufficient for the evaluated recovery horizon and episode subset.

- Null or ambiguous pattern: No detectable incremental history information would support sufficiency only for the specified state variables, event-category treatment, seasonal conditioning, recovery horizon, and observed episode subset; it would not establish universal state sufficiency or absence of atmospheric memory.
