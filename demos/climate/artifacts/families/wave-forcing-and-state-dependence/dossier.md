# Wave forcing, circulation response, and state-dependent feedback

This is a development rejection dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.

## Question Family

Separates two temporal interpretations of wave–mean-flow interaction: forcing as an antecedent of circulation change and background circulation as a conditioner of the response to forcing.

## Scientific Motivation

Wave and eddy activity may actively precede circulation transitions, while the circulation state may simultaneously regulate wave propagation and the persistence of the response; covariation alone cannot distinguish these directions.

## Why This Family Was Not Carried Forward

Neither active variant can be operationalized without changing protected scientific meaning: the package is limited to four unlabeled derived arrays on a one-dimensional 60 degrees North vertical grid, lacks the regional and remote-mode fields required by the variants, and does not document an exact pressure-coordinate or calendar interpretation. What would make this answerable: Provide an event-ready reanalysis product with authoritative time/calendar, pressure-coordinate, units, sign, and missing-data metadata plus variables sufficient to define sudden-stratospheric-warming onset independently of forcing. Provide longitude-resolved wave and circulation fields, or predeclared validated regional precursor-pathway classifications, to distinguish Ural from Aleutian pathways and split from displacement morphology. Provide matched ENSO and QBO indices or the fields and documented method required to derive them, together with a documented pressure-level zonal-wind product spanning 100–10 hPa.

## Variant Outcomes

- Variant 1 (Pathway- and morphology-stratified forcing-first sequence test): Within Northern Hemisphere extended-winter sudden stratospheric warmings, does independently diagnosed eddy forcing follow a prespecified forcing-first vertical sequence—upward planetary-wave propagation, stratospheric eddy forcing, vortex response, and only then downward circulation propagation—more often than matched reverse-order sequences, after separating split and displacement morphologies, Ural and Aleutian precursor pathways, background vortex states, and ordinary versus extreme forcing episodes?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant tests whether an independently diagnosed forcing sequence precedes a defined sudden-stratospheric-warming response and defeats reverse-order explanations within morphology and pathway strata. The sibling instead holds forcing episodes conceptually comparable and asks whether different antecedent circulation states modify their subsequent outcomes. The discriminating observation is A forcing-first interpretation would require, within each prespecified morphology and pathway stratum, an independently timed sequence in which anomalous upward EP-flux propagation reaches the lower stratosphere before diagnosed eddy forcing, the eddy forcing precedes the stratospheric vortex response, and downward circulation propagation occurs afterward. That complete sequence must be more reproducible than matched cases with vortex-response-first or downward-propagation-first ordering and than comparisons matched on seasonal and antecedent vortex background. Ordinary and extreme forcing episodes must be evaluated separately under the hypothesis that they express the same ordering at different amplitudes; failure of that agreement would indicate distinct regimes rather than support a pooled mechanism.
  - Planning disposition: Rejected dataset mismatch. The requested within-SSW sequence test requires split-versus-displacement morphology and Ural-versus-Aleutian precursor pathways, but the documented 60 degrees North one-dimensional package has no horizontal structure or regional fields. Its unlabeled arrays and unresolved time, pressure, unit, and sign semantics also cannot establish independent SSW onset, forcing timing, or the requested vertical sequence without substituting different constructs.
  - What would support or weaken it: If the complete ordering exceeds reverse-order and background-matched alternatives within multiple morphology and pathway strata and is concordant across ordinary and extreme episodes, it would support a conditional predictive forcing-first description and identify where the same proposed mechanism may span forcing intensities. If vortex changes systematically precede altered propagation or diagnosed forcing, or if matched reverse-order sequences are equally or more reproducible, the result would constrain forcing-first interpretations and favor flow-controlled wave propagation or common-background evolution. If no ordering is stable after stratification and background matching, the evidence would leave directionality unresolved and imply that sequence meaning is pathway-, morphology-, state-, or intensity-dependent rather than population-wide.
- Variant 2 (Antecedent vertical-structure susceptibility test under matched forcing): Among wave or eddy-forcing episodes matched on magnitude, wave spectrum and flux characteristics, duration, prior forcing history, and seasonal context, does the high-latitude 100–10 hPa zonal-mean wind structure during days 14–7 before onset condition transition probability, persistence duration, and recovery rate?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant assumes forcing episodes can be defined and asks whether matched episodes have different transition probabilities, persistence durations, and recovery rates across a specified pre-onset vertical wind structure. Its sibling instead asks whether forcing anomalies temporally precede circulation transitions at all. The discriminating observation is Support for state susceptibility would require that episodes sufficiently matched on forcing magnitude, wave/eddy spectrum or flux characteristics, duration, prior forcing history, and seasonal context show a reproducible contrast between weak and strong vertically coherent 100–10 hPa westerly profiles measured during days 14–7 before onset: specifically, different transition probabilities followed by differences in persistence duration and recovery timing or rate. ENSO, QBO, and analogous modes are excluded from the state definition and treated as conditioning variables and alternative representations in robustness comparisons, not assumed to be mediators. The contrast must remain after their conditioning; otherwise remote-mode modulation or forcing differences remain favored explanations.
  - Planning disposition: Rejected operationalization failure. The protected state definition requires a 100–10 hPa zonal-mean-wind profile and the discriminating contrast requires conditioning on ENSO and QBO after matched forcing. The authorized package provides no pressure coordinate, ENSO/QBO series, or documented derivation route, and its array semantics remain unresolved; replacing these with height-indexed or unconditioned proxies would materially alter the question.
  - What would support or weaken it: A stable matched-forcing contrast in transition probability, persistence duration, and recovery rate would support a conditional susceptibility account in which an explicitly defined antecedent vertical wind structure helps organize the trajectory following forcing, distinct from variation in the mean effect of a prescribed external perturbation across ENSO/QBO phases. If the apparent vertical-state contrast disappears after forcing histories, season, and remote modes are conditioned, the result would favor unequal-forcing or ENSO/QBO-related explanations over an independent vertical-state susceptibility interpretation. Weak, imprecise, or definition-sensitive contrasts would leave susceptibility unresolved and suggest that the proposed lead window, vertical-state representation, or response classes require refinement rather than establishing equivalence across states.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1, not carried forward (rejected dataset mismatch): Diagnosed eddy forcing is a recurrent antecedent of the stratospheric vortex response within particular warming morphologies and precursor pathways. Vortex evolution begins first and changes the propagation or absorption of planetary waves, producing an apparent forcing precursor. Seasonal stage or another slowly varying background state jointly organizes wave propagation, diagnosed forcing, and vortex evolution. The ordering differs between split and displacement warmings or between Ural and Aleutian precursor pathways rather than representing one mechanism. Ordinary and extreme forcing episodes belong to distinct regimes, so an ordering found only for extremes does not generalize to ordinary episodes. Apparent ordering conflates upward EP-flux propagation with local EP-flux divergence and subsequent downward circulation propagation. Variant 2, not carried forward (rejected operationalization failure): The specified antecedent vertical wind structure genuinely modifies susceptibility to comparable wave or eddy forcing. Residual differences in forcing magnitude, wavenumber spectrum, flux characteristics, duration, or prior forcing history produce the apparent state contrast. Seasonal stage jointly structures the antecedent wind profile, forcing, and subsequent response. ENSO, QBO, or analogous remote modes explain the contrast independently of, or provide an alternative representation of, the proposed vertical state. The antecedent wind profile is already responding to developing forcing, so the comparison does not isolate a pre-existing state. Threshold-based transition and recovery categories exaggerate differences along a continuous response trajectory.
