## Why this question matters

Asks whether nominally similar extreme disruptions share a common lifecycle or divide into scientifically meaningful onset and recovery pathways.

The scientific tension is:

Extreme polar-vortex disruptions may be amplified members of ordinary variability with a common lifecycle, or they may comprise heterogeneous dynamical pathways whose onset and recovery cannot be summarized by one composite.

## Variant 1: Conditional added-value test of antecedent forcing-sequence heterogeneity

### Why it matters

Establishing added pathway information would show when morphology-based composites omit recurrent dynamical organization and could improve interpretation of how preconditioning progresses into vortex decline. Failure to establish it would constrain claims that sequence-derived groups represent additional physical pathways.

### Original and refined question

**Original Question Scientist proposal**

Do extreme polar-vortex disruptions separate into recurrent onset pathways distinguished by the vertical timing and composition of antecedent wave and eddy forcing?

**Post-novelty revised proposal**

Conditional on established split/displacement morphology and DD/DS/SS wavenumber-evolution classes, do extreme polar-vortex disruptions exhibit reproducible onset pathways that cut across or subdivide those classes, defined by the vertical ordering of planetary-wave forcing, gravity-wave drag, and resolved eddy forcing, and that add discrimination of subsequent onset-transition timing?

### Scientific stakes

**Discriminating observation**

Support would require groups defined by the ordered vertical transitions among planetary-wave forcing, gravity-wave drag, and an explicitly documented resolved-eddy forcing measure—for example, differences in whether lower-atmospheric planetary-wave enhancement precedes or follows upper-level drag changes and in when these give way to stratopause deceleration and downward-easterly development. The groups must reproduce in separated event subsets and retain discrimination of transition timing or onset evolution after conditioning on split/displacement and DD/DS/SS type and accounting for onset alignment, event strength, duration, and seasonal context. Recovery of established type contrasts alone would not qualify.

**What possible outcomes would mean**

- Positive pattern: Reproducible cross-cutting or within-type groups that retain added discrimination of a transition property would support an onset-pathway dimension complementary to established morphology and wavenumber-evolution classifications, qualifying single-composite accounts of disruption onset.
- Negative pattern: Failure to find stable sequence groups after conditioning and accounting for the specified alternatives would indicate a lack of evidence for additional pathway structure beyond recurrent common preconditioning and established split/displacement and DD/DS/SS distinctions; it would not establish a blanket single common-onset mechanism.
- Null or ambiguous pattern: If conclusions depend strongly on forcing definitions, alignment, event classification, subset composition, or uncertain transition identification, the evidence would remain insufficient to distinguish extra pathway structure from known type contrasts and methodological instability.
