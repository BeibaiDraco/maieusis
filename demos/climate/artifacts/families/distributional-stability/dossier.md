# Historical stability of ordinary and extreme vortex variability

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Tests whether historical variation appears as a broad distributional shift or as selective change in extremes. One variant emphasizes circulation-state tails; the other emphasizes forcing-history tails and their relationship to circulation outcomes.

## Scientific Motivation

Multi-decadal stratospheric variability may be historically stable apart from ordinary sampling fluctuations, may shift approximately uniformly, or may change disproportionately in consequential tails.

## Variant Plans

- Variant 1 (Circulation-distribution tail stability): Across the multi-decadal record, do extreme weak and strong vortex states change in step with the central circulation distribution, or do the tails exhibit disproportionate historical variation?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant treats the circulation distribution itself as the outcome and contrasts its center with weak and strong tails; it does not center the analysis on extreme forcing histories. The discriminating observation is Disproportionate tail variation would be supported if weak or strong extremes change relative to the center in a manner inconsistent with a common additive shift and stable under reasonable state and period definitions.
  - Planned analysis: The shared 43-year ubar array and seasonal/anomaly products support a pre-specified comparison of the circulation center with weak and strong tails, conditional on resolving variable semantics and calendar metadata.
    - Refined question: Across the documented multi-decadal ubar record, do pre-specified weak and strong circulation tails vary relative to the central distribution more than is compatible with a common location shift?

    - Unit of observation: A pre-specified ubar value at one validated height representation and one validated seasonal time cell.
    - Unit of inference: Year-block or winter-season block, with within-season observations treated as serially dependent rather than independent replicates.
    - Hierarchy and dependence: Model repeated cells nested within year and season, preserve autocorrelation with block resampling or clustered uncertainty, and avoid treating adjacent six-hourly slots or heights as independent evidence.
    - Analysis strategy: Resolve ubar sign and representation from documentation, then choose a pre-specified physically motivated level or vertical summary without target-outcome tuning. Use transient anomalies as the primary seasonal-adjusted representation and compare against analysis fields with explicit seasonal adjustment as a robustness representation. Define weak and strong tails from fixed pooled reference quantiles or a pre-declared extreme-value threshold, and define the center by pre-declared central quantiles. Estimate indexed-year or pre-specified period contrasts in center, weak-tail, and strong-tail location and scale; test tail-versus-center interaction terms under a common-shift null formulation. Repeat under pre-specified season windows, level summaries, tail cutoffs, and state-mixture adjustments to distinguish shape change from changing seasonal or recurrent-state composition.
    - Candidate estimands: Difference in historical change between each fixed tail quantile and the median of seasonally adjusted ubar. Difference in tail exceedance probability or tail scale across pre-specified early and late indexed periods, standardized to the central-distribution change.
    - Validation strategy: Before target estimation, verify coordinate decoding, missingness, ana-minus-sea versus tran consistency within a small predeclared structural check, and recovery of known synthetic common-shift versus tail-scale scenarios using the planned estimator.
    - Split strategy: Use blocked year-level resampling and leave-contiguous-year-block-out sensitivity analysis; do not randomly split temporally adjacent cells.
    - Diagnostics: Coverage and missingness by height, month, time slot, and year index. Autocorrelation, effective sample-size, and tail-count diagnostics by pre-specified seasonal window. Influence of individual year blocks and sensitivity to observing-system-era or breakpoint proxies when documentation permits.
    - Negative controls: A permutation of year-block labels that preserves within-block dependence, used only to check the analysis pipeline's null calibration. A central-quantile contrast that should not be labeled as tail-specific change when all quantiles move together.
    - Positive controls: Synthetic injected common-shift and tail-scale changes, which the estimator should distinguish before use on the target fields.
    - Alternative explanations: Changing seasonal sampling or padded-calendar treatment. Changing mixture of recurrent circulation states rather than within-state distributional change. Reanalysis assimilation or observing-system discontinuities. Unresolved ubar sign, unit, or vertical-representation conventions.
    - Predicted result patterns: A tail-specific interpretation is supported only if tail-versus-center contrasts persist across pre-specified tail definitions, representations, and dependence-aware uncertainty procedures. A common-shift interpretation is favored if weak and strong tail changes track central changes and tail interaction estimates remain unstable or near the common-shift pattern. No distributional conclusion is warranted if coverage, calendar, or provenance checks fail or tail contrasts are definition-sensitive.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: Apparent tail change could result from altered measurement or assimilation conditions rather than atmospheric change. Response variation could reflect changing background susceptibility rather than altered forcing potency. An extreme aggregate forcing measure may combine distinct vertical or temporal structures.
