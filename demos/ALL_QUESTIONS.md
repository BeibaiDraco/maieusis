## How to read this

Maieusis was pointed at three real datasets — brain-wide recordings from mice making decisions, a
single session of macaque motor cortex during reaching, and four decades of the polar stratosphere —
and asked what would be worth asking of each. It read the field's papers, was handed a deliberately
coarse description of the dataset, and wrote six questions per run: twenty-four in all. It then
asked each question twice, in two deliberately different ways, so 48 attempts.

**Four demonstrations, three datasets.** The brain-wide recordings appear twice, and the pair is the
most direct thing on this page. One run was told what to think about — a single declared anchor,
*noise correlations* — and the other was told nothing and worked out its own scope from the
dataset's own description. Same recordings, same twelve papers, same models. They produced twelve
different questions, with no family in common — not one shared title. Individual questions do
reach the same ground from opposite directions; what the anchor changed is which contrasts each
run organised its six families around. Reading the two side by side is the only way to see it.

Prior-art review stopped eight of the 48 before planning began. Most of the
rest went to a coding agent that opened whatever the dataset exposed —
documentation and file inventories, schemas and metadata, and in some families real data samples —
and tried to turn each one into a study plan that would survive contact with it. **Twenty-seven
became plans. Thirteen were closed once the planner had seen the data and the
task.** Every family's page states how deep its own inspection went.

**All 48 are on this page.** The refusals are not gaps we forgot to fill — they are
the part you cannot get from a system that shows you only what worked.

> **Nothing here was run.** No analysis was executed, no effect was measured, no hypothesis was
> tested, and no page below reports a result. Every plan was checked by a second model, on a
> different provider, independently of the model that wrote it — never by a human expert — and then
> stopped there.

**The reviewer agreed with the Owner every time, and you should know that before you weigh the
word "independent".** Twenty families carry both decisions: eighteen were accepted by both, two
were sent back for revision by both, and the reviewer overturned nothing. Read that in whichever
direction the evidence supports for you — two models reaching the same verdict from different
vendors on evidence they did not share, or a check that has not yet been observed to bite. It is
one release over four runs, which is too little to settle it. The disagreement rate is the number
this design would have to be judged on, and publishing it while it reads well is the only way the
number means anything later.

Two caveats before the science. The literature behind these questions is visibly draft: much of it
was read as abstracts and metadata rather than full text. Twenty of the family pages open with a
**Data basis** banner saying exactly that, and each demonstration's topic-evidence summary counts
how many of its open gaps rest on abstract-only sources. Every one of the
48 went through prior-art review within a recorded scope, but no question is claimed
to be novel — no search proves absence. Each demo page names the run behind it.

## Before you open one

**Every question was asked twice, on purpose.** Maieusis calls the two versions *variants*. They are
not a first draft and a revision: they are two ways of asking one thing, built so that a result
supporting one would count against the other. Each entry below shows both, with a short name and the
question as the run itself worded it. A group of two is a *family*, and the small italic line under
each heading is the run's own name and number for it — that is what you will see on the pages the
entry links to.

**Prior-art review sharpens a question before it is planned.** The wording you read below is the
proposal-stage wording. Where the review found a scholarly record sitting close, it required the
question to be made specific enough to be told apart from that work, and the sharpened version is
the one the planner actually received. Several closures below turn on a term the sharpening added.
Most reading guides print both wordings side by side, the second labelled *post-novelty revised
proposal*; where a guide shows only one wording, none was recorded.

**Seven things could happen to a family.** The name in bold is the label the run assigns; the
sentence after it is what the label means.

- **Plan developed (provisional)** — both versions got a plan. *Provisional* means an independent
  reviewer signed off — a second model, on a different provider, not a human expert — and that
  nothing was executed. Nine families.
- **Mixed family** — one version got a plan and the other was closed during planning, once the
  planner had inspected the dataset's schemas and metadata and read the task description. The closed
  one stays on the page with its reason. Three families.
- **Deferred on prior-art grounds** — one version got a plan and the other was stopped *before*
  planning, by prior-art review. Two different findings stop a version here, and each family's own
  disposition line says which: a real scholarly record — a paper or a preprint — sitting close
  enough that the question had to be made distinguishable from it, or a search that could not
  retrieve enough evidence to judge the question either way. Where a prior is named, a model's
  impression is not enough: it has to resolve to a DOI-identified identity, and it does not mean
  the prior answers the question. Six families.
- **Stopped before planning on prior-art grounds** — the same review, and *every* version stopped
  there, so the family has no plan and no planning record at all. It is not a rejection: nobody
  inspected the data and judged the question unanswerable; the question was closed before it ever
  reached that stage. Its question page carries only the bare disposition, because the line that
  names a prior is written by the shortlist stage this family never reached; what each version
  ran into is on the run's prior-art page instead. One family.
- **Scientific rejection terminal** — the family produced no plan at all. Every version that reached
  the planner was closed there, and the run names the construct that was missing rather than
  substituting a proxy for it. This is a scientific answer — not a failure, not a crash, and not an
  abandoned branch. Two families.
- **Service warning** — no plan survives, and the reason is the machinery rather than the science.
  The run says so instead of presenting a partial family as a complete one. One family.
- **Validation warning** — the planner did its work and the material it returned could not be fully
  validated on the way back in, so no plan is claimed. Like a service warning this is machinery, not
  science, and it is named rather than dressed up as a scientific closure. two
  families. What went wrong in this set is written down: the planner revised its own evidence files
  under new names and left the earlier drafts beside them, and both copies were read back.

**Each entry ends with up to three links, richest first.**

1. *The question in full* — the scientific background, the competing explanations, the observation
   that would tell them apart, and what a positive, negative, or null result would mean.
2. *The reading guide* — sets what the proposal stage assumed beside what the planner actually saw
   in the data. It is the only place the Question Owner and the independent reviewer speak in their
   own words.
3. *The full planning record* — every control, estimand, diagnostic and stated limit, and the full
   reasoning for any version closed during planning.

Neither of the last two contains the other, so read both. Two exceptions are worth knowing before
you click. A version stopped by prior-art review **never reached planning**, so it appears in no
planning record at all; its reasoning — which prior was found, and what would have to change —
lives in the first link. Eight of the 48 are in that state, and each is
flagged where it appears. And a family whose *every* version stopped there has no reading guide at
all, because no planning branch was opened to write one about: that entry offers two links, not
three.

Other labels travel with these plans: how deeply the planner got into the data, what kind of claim a
design could support, how the supporting literature was read. All of them, with their permitted
values, are in [reading the labels](../docs/LABELS.md).

## Mice making decisions: the IBL Brain-Wide Map, asked about noise

Standardized Neuropixels recordings from mice performing a sensory decision task, with the same
protocol repeated across many laboratories. This run was given one anchor — *noise correlations*
— and told to develop questions around it. Every family below is downstream of that one phrase,
and the next demonstration is the same recordings with no phrase at all.

Three of these six carry a warning rather than a plan. Two are a defect in how the planner's
returned files were read back, one is a transport failure, and none of the three is a finding
about the data. They stay on the page because a demonstration that quietly dropped them would
be showing you a cleaner run than the one that happened.

### 1. Functional alignment of shared variability during perceptual decisions

*Family 001: Functional alignment of shared variability during perceptual decisions*

Noise correlations may be largely nonspecific fluctuations, or their geometry may selectively align with population dimensions carrying sensory evidence or impending choice. These alternatives can produce similar average correlation magnitudes but different functional interpretations.

1. [**Sensory-code alignment branch**](ibl/artifacts/questions/question_families_detailed.md#variant-001001-sensory-code-alignment-branch) — Is shared trial-to-trial variability selectively aligned with sensory-evidence representations, and does that alignment predict reduced or preserved stimulus discriminability beyond overall noise-correlation magnitude?
2. [**Choice and behavior alignment branch**](ibl/artifacts/questions/question_families_detailed.md#variant-001002-choice-and-behavior-alignment-branch) — Is shared trial-to-trial variability selectively aligned with choice-related population structure, and does that alignment predict choices or response-time variation after distinguishing sensory and embodied alternatives?

**Outcome: Mixed family.** One version got a plan; the other was closed during planning, once the planner had inspected the dataset. The closed one stays on the page with its reason.

*Family 002: Input-linked versus internally generated origins of correlated variability*

Limited sensory input, circuit organization, and internal fluctuations can all generate information-limiting correlations, but they predict different changes in covariance around sensory input and decision formation.

1. [**Sensory-input origin branch**](ibl/artifacts/questions/question_families_detailed.md#variant-002001-sensory-input-origin-branch) — Does correlated variability track the strength and timing of sensory drive in a manner consistent with an input-linked origin?

[**Persistent internal-state origin branch**](ibl/artifacts/questions/question_families_detailed.md#variant-002002-persistent-internal-state-origin-branch) — Does correlated variability persist beyond immediate sensory drive and predict subsequent decision timing in a manner consistent with internally generated state or decision dynamics?

### 3. Embodied and internal-state explanations of apparent noise correlations

*Family 003: Embodied and internal-state explanations of apparent noise correlations*

1. ## The same mice, asked nothing: the IBL Brain-Wide Map in open mode

The same recordings, the same twelve papers, the same models — and no declared topic. Instead of
being handed an anchor, this run read the dataset's own description and derived its own scope,
then asked six questions inside it.

Put the two IBL pages side by side and the difference is the whole argument. Neither list is a
subset of the other and no family appears in both. Declaring *noise correlations* did not narrow
the run to a smaller version of the open one; it moved it somewhere else. That is what a
research intent is for, and it is also why an anchor is a commitment rather than a hint: a term
that is wrong for the dataset steers six questions away from the ones worth asking, and nothing
downstream will put them back.

## The polar stratosphere: ERA5

Four decades of daily zonal-mean wind at 60N, derived from ERA5, asked in open mode. It is a
deliberately thin description — one latitude, no longitude — and it is the leg where the
thinness is most visible in what the planner said it could and could not do.

The refusal here is worth the click even though it is not what the page leads with. It asked
whether rapid vortex weakening is organized mainly by anomalous wave forcing or by the
susceptibility of the state the vortex was already in — a live division in the literature, and
a reasonable thing to ask. Separating those two accounts needs the horizontal structure of the
forcing, and this record is a single latitude circle with longitude already averaged away. The
planner named the missing construct and stopped, rather than substituting a proxy that would
have answered something else.
