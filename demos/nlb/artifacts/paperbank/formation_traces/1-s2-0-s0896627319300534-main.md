## Starting background

- Interareal cortical interactions are considered important for brain function, and prior studies established relationships between activity in connected areas using pairwise measures, local-field-potential relationships, or trial-averaged activity.
- Population recordings and dimensionality-reduction methods can reveal structure that is not apparent from single-neuron or pairwise analyses, but the relevant source dimensions for communication between cortical areas remained unspecified.
- Prior V1–V2 work provided evidence that coordinated activity and population-level coupling are related to corticocortical signaling, while not determining whether communication uses the full source population or a selective subset of dimensions.

## Unresolved gap

The provided evidence supports a specific unresolved question: whether activity transmitted from one cortical area to another is distributed broadly across the source population or selectively organized in a lower-dimensional subspace, and whether that interareal structure differs from interactions among subpopulations within the source area. Existing pairwise or trial-averaged approaches did not resolve this population-level distinction.

## Dataset opportunity

The study had simultaneous population recordings from macaque V1 and V2 across repeated stimulus presentations, yielding trial-matched fluctuations in source and target activity. The V1 recordings also supported a held-out within-V1 comparison, allowing interareal interactions to be contrasted with within-area population interactions.

Simultaneous trial-matched recordings make it possible to relate source-population fluctuations to target-population fluctuations rather than relying only on pairwise association or trial averages. Repeated presentations provide the fluctuations needed for prediction, and the within-V1 comparator helps distinguish a selective interareal communication structure from generic low dimensionality or target-population complexity.

## Resulting question

How do trial-to-trial fluctuations in a source cortical population relate to activity in a simultaneously recorded target population, and does the source-to-target relationship operate through a selective low-dimensional communication subspace rather than through the full source activity space or a structure comparable to within-area interactions?

## Scientific consequence

If a small, distinct subset of source dimensions predicts target activity nearly as well as the full source population, the result supports selective routing of population activity between areas and motivates distinguishing predictive dimensions from dominant or private dimensions. If prediction requires most source dimensions, or if the interareal structure resembles within-area coupling, the case for selective communication is weakened. Either outcome would clarify whether interareal coupling reflects generic shared activity or a more specific population-level organization.

The question is scientifically valuable because it converts the broad problem of cortical communication into a testable account of which population activity patterns are related across areas. It provides a population-level alternative to interpreting communication through isolated pairwise relationships and offers a way to study selective routing without assuming that the most prominent source fluctuations are the ones transmitted. As a PaperBank pattern, it illustrates how a dataset with simultaneous repeated population measurements can be used to resolve the structure, not merely the existence, of inter-system interaction.
