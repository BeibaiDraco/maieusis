## Resulting question

How are trial-to-trial fluctuations in a source V1 population related to simultaneously recorded V2 population activity, and are V1–V2 interactions carried by a restricted communication subspace whose dimensions and dimensionality differ from matched within-V1 interactions and from the dominant shared fluctuations in V1?

## Scientific consequence

If only a subset of source dimensions predicts the target, interareal communication would be consistent with selective routing rather than transmission of the full source population state; if most dimensions predict the target, a broad population-transfer account would remain more plausible. Alignment with dominant within-V1 fluctuations would suggest that communication largely follows the strongest local population modes, whereas distinct predictive dimensions would indicate that local amplitude or shared variability is not equivalent to interareal transmission. A difference from matched V1–V1 interactions would further support an area-specific interaction structure, although the supplied evidence does not establish a universal property across cortical systems.

The question links a broad problem—how interconnected brain areas communicate—to an observable population-level distinction between dominant local activity and activity that is effectively related to another area. Both positive and negative outcomes would refine how neural signals are conceptualized: either as broadly distributed across a source population or as selectively routed through a lower-dimensional structure.
