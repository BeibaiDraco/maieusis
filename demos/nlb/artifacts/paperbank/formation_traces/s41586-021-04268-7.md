## Unresolved gap

## Resulting question

Does the joint activity of grid cells within an individual module occupy a two-dimensional toroidal manifold, and are that manifold and the cells' toroidal firing locations preserved across environments and between wakefulness and sleep in the manner predicted by continuous-attractor models, rather than by alternative feedforward accounts?

## Scientific consequence

A toroidal manifold that maps consistently to physical position and persists across environmental conditions and sleep would provide population-level evidence for the organization predicted by two-dimensional continuous-attractor models and constrain how environmental inputs interact with the representation. Failure to find the topology, or instability of cell locations across conditions, would weaken that interpretation and leave feedforward, geometry-dependent, or additional mechanisms more viable. Thus both positive and negative outcomes would change the mechanistic interpretation of the grid code.

The question links a mechanistic theory of neural computation to an observable population-level signature rather than inferring mechanism from single-cell firing patterns alone. It is scientifically valuable because it asks whether a theoretically predicted global organization survives changes in sensory context and brain state, and because it illustrates how high-density recordings can make previously inaccessible properties of a neural state space empirically testable.
