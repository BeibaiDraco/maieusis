## Resulting question

What dimensionality and geometric structure characterize stimulus-evoked population responses in mouse visual cortex, and does the population eigenspectrum depend primarily on natural-image statistics, stimulus dimensionality, or a general smoothness constraint on neural coding?

## Scientific consequence

Evidence for high-dimensional, nearly uncorrelated responses would support an efficient-coding interpretation, whereas persistent correlations and a structured power-law spectrum would support a code organized around smooth, robust representations. If changing stimulus statistics leaves the neural power law while changing stimulus dimensionality changes its exponent, the result would favor a general coding constraint over inheritance from natural-image statistics. Either outcome would refine how population correlations, dimensionality, and robustness are related.

The question links a long-standing theoretical conflict to a measurable geometric property of large neural populations and tests whether that property is stimulus-specific or a broader organizing constraint. It is scientifically valuable because it can distinguish competing explanations of population structure rather than merely describe correlations, and because it connects coding efficiency with the smoothness and generalization of neural representations.
