## Resulting question

Does diversity in neuronal firing rates predict lower-dimensional trial-to-trial population covariability in real neuronal populations; under what circuit-scale conditions does this relationship persist or break down, especially for structured inputs, recurrent networks, and populations pooled across brain areas; and does the combination of diverse mean responses and low-dimensional variability improve stimulus discrimination?

## Scientific consequence

If the relationship persists across conditions, species, and single-area populations, it would support a circuit-level link between mean-response geometry and covariance geometry rather than a purely uncoupled-neuron explanation. If structured inputs or interareal pooling disrupt it, the relationship would have a bounded local-circuit scope and its failure could provide indirect information about correlation or connectivity structure. If diverse responses and low-dimensional variability jointly improve discriminability, the result would connect the mechanistic relationship to neural coding; if they do not, the relationship would still describe population organization without implying a decoding benefit.

The question links two central dimensions of population activity—what neurons encode on average and how their trial-to-trial fluctuations are organized—and tests whether their association reflects a plausible circuit mechanism. Its value lies in distinguishing a general organizing principle from a context-dependent one, while also showing how covariance geometry may matter for population coding.
