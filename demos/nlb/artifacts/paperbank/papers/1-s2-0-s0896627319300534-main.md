## Question the system extracted

How are trial-to-trial fluctuations of population responses in V1 related to simultaneously recorded population responses in V2, and do V1-V2 interactions occur through a low-dimensional communication subspace rather than through the full V1 population activity?

## The scientific contrast

Between-area V1-V2 population interactions versus interactions between subpopulations within V1, with an additional contrast between activity dimensions predictive of V2 and the largest shared fluctuations within V1.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- V1-V2 interactions could involve all or most dimensions of V1 population activity rather than a restricted subspace.
- The apparently lower-dimensional V1-V2 interaction could arise because the V2 target population has intrinsically lower-dimensional activity than the target V1 population.
- The predictive dimensions could simply coincide with the largest shared or dominant fluctuations within V1.
- The lower-dimensional structure could be specific to the V1-V2 recording rather than a general property of inter-areal interactions.

What would tell them apart: A communication-subspace account predicts that only a small subset of V1 population activity dimensions predicts V2 fluctuations, that these dimensions are distinct from the largest shared V1 fluctuations, and that the pattern differs from matched within-V1 interactions.

## Background and motivation

- Most brain functions involve interactions among multiple distinct cortical areas or nuclei, and visual processing in primates requires relaying signals across distinct cortical areas.
- Previous studies related activity between pairs of neurons or between a neuronal population in one area and a single neuron or local field potential in another area, but these approaches did not elucidate how spiking activity of neuronal populations—the signals thought to encode information in the brain—is related across areas.
- It was unknown whether all or only a subset of the dimensions of population activity are related across brain areas, and which dimensions are related.
- The largest activity patterns in a source area are not necessarily the patterns matched across areas; activity patterns related across areas can be distinct from the largest fluctuations shared among neurons within the source area.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question distinguishes a broad correlation or full-population account of inter-areal communication from a selective-routing account. It can identify which population activity patterns are effectively relayed between cortical areas and thereby clarify a population-level mechanism relevant to how brain areas interact.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Simultaneous recordings of source and target neuronal populations in visual cortical areas, including comparisons between V1-V2 interactions and interactions between subpopulations within V1. Responses were measured across repeated presentations of drifting sinusoidal gratings of different orientations.

- Population: Anesthetized macaque monkeys, with additional paired V1-V4 recordings in awake animals reported for generalization.
- Measurements: Spike counts in 100 ms bins, Trial-to-trial response fluctuations, Pairwise correlations, Multivariate linear-regression prediction, Predictive dimensions and low-dimensional communication subspaces, Population activity dimensionality
