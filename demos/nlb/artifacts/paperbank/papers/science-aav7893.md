## Question the system extracted

Does ongoing spontaneous neural activity encode multidimensional aspects of the animal's behavior, how broadly is this behaviorally related activity distributed across the brain, and how does it interact with sensory-stimulus representations?

## The scientific contrast

Whether apparently spontaneous or noisy neural activity is better explained as random or stimulus-related variability, versus structured activity encoding ongoing multidimensional behavior and behavioral state; and whether sensory and behavioral information occupy overlapping or orthogonal neural dimensions.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Spontaneous activity may be random noise.
- Spontaneous activity may reflect recall or recapitulation of prior sensory experience.
- Spontaneous activity may encode ongoing behavioral and cognitive variables.
- One-dimensional arousal measures may account for the relevant behaviorally related activity, versus higher-dimensional facial and motor behavior accounting for more of it.

What would tell them apart: If spontaneous activity encodes ongoing multidimensional behavior, behavioral variables extracted from facial videography and other motor measures should predict reliable neural population dimensions, including during spontaneous periods, and behaviorally related activity should be identifiable across brain regions. Comparing spontaneous and stimulus periods and projecting activity into separate subspaces distinguishes behavioral from sensory information.

## Background and motivation

- Neuronal populations in sensory cortex exhibit variable responses to sensory stimuli and also show intricate spontaneous activity without external sensory input.
- Spontaneous activity has been proposed to represent random noise, recall of prior experience, or ongoing behavioral and cognitive variables.
- Previous work linked spontaneous neural activity to behavior through measures such as running speed and pupil diameter, but the paper considers whether spontaneous activity contains higher-dimensional behavioral information.
- The paper hypothesizes that, in the absence of sensory inputs, the brain produces structured patterns of activity and investigates whether these patterns exist even in primary sensory cortices.
- The representation of sensory stimuli and behavioral variables can be mixed in the same neurons, raising the question of how ongoing behavior relates to stimulus responses.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question determines whether spontaneous variability in sensory cortex and across the brain is merely noise or carries structured information about ongoing behavior. It also clarifies how behavioral state is represented alongside sensory information, which bears on interpretation of stimulus responses and trial-to-trial variability.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Spontaneous behavior during ongoing recordings, with additional periods of darkness, gray-screen activity, and visual stimulation including flashed natural images and classical grating stimuli.

- Population: Awake mice, including mice free to run on an air-floating ball and mice freely rotating a wheel with their front paws.
- Measurements: Ongoing and stimulus-evoked neuronal activity, Facial motion and multidimensional behavioral components, Running speed, pupil diameter, whisking, and arousal-related variables, Reliable variance, shared variance components, stimulus decoding, and behavior-based prediction of neural activity
