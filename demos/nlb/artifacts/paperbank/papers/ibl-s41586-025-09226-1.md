# Paper — Brain-wide representations of prior information in mouse decision-making

## Question the system extracted

Where and how is prior information about the likely side of a visual stimulus represented across the mouse brain, and do its spatial distribution and inter-area dynamics support a brain-wide Bayesian-network model rather than a model in which the prior is incorporated only in decision-making areas?

## The scientific contrast

A distributed, brain-wide representation with bidirectional inter-area communication versus a representation restricted mainly to late decision-making regions.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- Prior information is encoded mainly or only in late decision-making areas.
- Prior information is available throughout the brain, including early sensory regions, as expected from a loopy Bayesian-network model with inference operating across areas.
- Apparent neural prior signals may instead reflect an embodied prior arising from body posture, movement, licking, whisking, or eye position.

What would tell them apart: Brain-wide recordings during the same task can test whether the prior is decodable across early sensory, motor, associative, cortical, and subcortical regions, and whether directed communication and feedback loops connect higher-order regions with early sensory areas. Zero-contrast trials and controls using video-derived behavioral features help distinguish prior-related signals from stimulus information and motor or eye-position confounds.

## Background and motivation

- Combining sensory information with prior knowledge through probabilistic inference is crucial for perception and cognition.
- In simple cases, the brain performs inference near-optimally according to Bayesian decision theory, and prior information has been reported in late-stage parietal, orbitofrontal, and prefrontal areas.
- A brain-wide Bayesian-network model would allow probabilistic inference in all brain regions and in multiple directions, whereas a late-stage model predicts that prior information is encoded only near decision-making areas.
- Prior information could therefore be represented throughout the brain, including low-level sensory areas, but existing evidence was collected from limited sets of areas using different tasks.
- The literature gives a contradictory and inconclusive perspective on whether prior information is encoded in early-processing regions, partly because prior probability can be confounded with reward probability in earlier task designs.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question would clarify whether prior information is a localized decision variable or a distributed signal communicated across the brain. It would also bear on whether neural implementations of Bayesian inference require recurrent interactions among sensory, motor, associative, cortical, and subcortical areas, rather than incorporation of prior information only near the final decision.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Mice moved a wheel to bring a peripheral visual grating to the centre of the screen. Stimulus contrast varied across trials, while the probability that the stimulus appeared on the right alternated between 0.2 and 0.8 in blocks of variable length. The block structure was uncued, allowing estimation of a subjective prior from trial history.

- Population: Mice trained to discriminate whether a visual grating appeared in the left or right visual field.
- Measurements: Choice behavior and psychometric curves, Neural decoding of the Bayes-optimal prior during the intertrial interval and after stimulus presentation, Region-level prior encoding across electrophysiological and widefield-imaging data, Granger-causality relationships and directed information-flow loops, Video-derived body posture, movement, whisking, licking, and eye-position features
