## Question the system extracted

Where and how is prior information about the likely side of a visual stimulus represented across the mouse brain, and is its representation restricted to decision-making areas or distributed across processing levels and communicating brain regions?

## The scientific contrast

A late-stage or decision-area account versus a brain-wide Bayesian-network account in which prior information is represented across sensory, associative, motor and subcortical regions and communicated through loops between areas.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- The prior may be encoded mainly in late decision-making areas rather than early sensory regions.
- The apparent neural prior may reflect an embodied prior arising from changes in body posture, movement or eye position rather than a neural representation of prior information itself.
- Prior-related neural activity may reflect stimulus identity or other task variables rather than the animal's subjective prior.

What would tell them apart: Evidence for prior decoding during the intertrial interval and on zero-contrast trials across early sensory, associative, motor and subcortical regions, together with cross-region feedback and controls for video-derived motor and eye-position features, would distinguish a distributed prior representation from a decision-area or embodied-confound account.

## Background and motivation

- Combining sensory information with prior knowledge through probabilistic inference is crucial for perception and cognition.
- Existing theoretical work on neural representations of Bayesian inference leaves unclear where and how prior knowledge is represented in the brain.
- One account predicts that prior information is encoded mainly in late processing or decision-making areas, whereas a brain-wide Bayesian-network account predicts that probabilistic inference can occur throughout the brain through communication between areas.
- Previous studies collectively provide a contradictory and inconclusive perspective on whether the prior is encoded in early processing regions, because they recorded limited sets of areas and used different tasks.
- The presence of uncued probabilistic blocks means that mice can form only a subjective estimate of the true block prior from trial history; therefore, prior encoding cannot be assessed simply by treating block identity as directly available to the animals.

## Why this question mattered

Determining whether prior information is represented only near decisions or throughout the brain constrains how Bayesian inference is implemented neurally. A distributed representation with inter-area loops would support a brain-wide network account, whereas a late and localized representation would support a more restricted decision-stage account.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Mice moved a visual grating to the centre of the screen by turning a wheel. The grating appeared in the left or right visual field, with the probability of a right-side stimulus alternating between 0.2 and 0.8 in uncued blocks of variable length; stimulus contrast varied across trials.

- Population: Mice performing a visual left-versus-right decision-making task; the electrophysiological dataset included recordings from 242 brain regions, and the widefield imaging data covered cortical regions.
- Measurements: Choice behaviour and accuracy, Neural activity during the intertrial interval and after stimulus onset, Decoding of the Bayes-optimal prior, Decoding of stimulus contrast and prior residuals, Body posture, whisking, licking, and eye position from video, Cross-region temporal relationships and Granger-causality-based information flow
