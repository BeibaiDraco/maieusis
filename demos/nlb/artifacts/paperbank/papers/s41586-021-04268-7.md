## Question the system extracted

Does the joint activity of grid cells within an individual module reside on a two-dimensional toroidal manifold, and are the manifold and individual cells’ toroidal firing locations maintained across environments and between wakefulness and sleep as predicted by continuous-attractor models?

## The scientific contrast

Intrinsic recurrent continuous-attractor dynamics versus alternative feedforward mechanisms, tested through the topology and cross-condition stability of grid-cell population activity.

Competing explanations the paper weighed:
- A two-dimensional continuous-attractor network predicts a toroidal population manifold, movement on that manifold corresponding to the animal’s trajectory, and stable cell locations across environments and brain states.
- Alternative feedforward models can generate grid-like activity but do not predict the same persistence of individual toroidal firing locations across environments and from wakefulness to sleep.
- The toroidal structure might reflect the encoded spatial variable or environmental geometry rather than intrinsic network organization; persistence despite environmental distortions and absence of external sensory inputs distinguishes these possibilities.

What would tell them apart: A toroidal barcode and low-dimensional manifold should appear in simultaneously recorded module activity; physical position should map onto the same internal manifold across environments; individual cells should retain toroidal firing locations across environments and sleep; and these observations should be better predicted by toroidal coordinates or continuous-attractor models than by shuffled, physical-position-only or feedforward alternatives.

## Background and motivation

- Grid cells fire in a characteristic hexagonal pattern and are organized in modules that collectively form a population code for the animal’s allocentric position.
- The correlation structure of the grid-cell population code is invariant across environments and behavioural states and is independent of specific sensory inputs.
- This invariance has pointed to intrinsic, recurrently connected continuous attractor networks as a possible substrate of the grid pattern.
- Continuous attractor models conceptualize neural representations as periodic one- or two-dimensional continua on a ring or torus, with activity stabilizing as a localized bump.
- Previous recordings with only a few dozen head-direction cells had been sufficient to demonstrate activity traversing a ring, but the much larger state space of grid-cell population activity had prevented the topology of its manifold from being determined.
- Environmental geometry can distort the grid pattern and the mapping between physical space and neural activity, so a topological rather than purely geometric characterization is needed.

## Why this question mattered

Answering the question would determine whether grid-cell population activity has the toroidal organization expected from two-dimensional continuous-attractor models, distinguish that mechanism from feedforward alternatives, and provide a population-level view of the network dynamics underlying the grid code.

## What the dataset offered (as the paper used it)

Rats foraged in a square open-field enclosure or on an elevated wagon-wheel track and also rested or slept in an opaque box; population activity was analysed across open-field and wagon-wheel environments, wakefulness, REM sleep and slow-wave sleep.

- Population: Freely moving rats with recordings from the medial entorhinal cortex, parasubiculum and surrounding areas; six grid-cell modules were analysed across four recording sessions in three rats.
- Measurements: Extracellular spikes from 7,671 single units, Grid-cell firing-rate maps, Population coactivation patterns, Persistent-cohomology barcodes and toroidal topology, Toroidal firing-field locations, Physical position, Head-direction and temporal firing characteristics
