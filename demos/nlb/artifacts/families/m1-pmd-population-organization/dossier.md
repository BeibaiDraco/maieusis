# Shared and selective population organization across M1 and PMd

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `sample_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Asks whether M1 and PMd express broadly shared movement geometry or complementary, selectively organized representations during delayed reaching.

## Scientific Motivation

Regional activity may reflect a common motor representation expressed across both areas or complementary organizations emphasizing different aspects of preparation and movement; regional decodability alone cannot decide between them.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: Cross-region correspondence is produced by shared direction, target, speed, elapsed-time, movement-onset, or task-epoch structure rather than trajectory-specific relations. A flexible alignment procedure imposes correspondence between genuinely different regional representations. An apparent mismatch reflects unequal condition sampling, population reliability, measurement quality, or regional metadata rather than specialization. The relevant cross-region relation is nonlinear or otherwise incompatible with the prespecified alignment even though both regions encode equivalent trajectory content. Variant 2: Both regions carry a shared directional code, with apparent crossover caused by differences in response magnitude, timing, reliability, or population sampling. PMd selectivity reflects target uncertainty or a conditional distribution over possible upcoming targets rather than instructed future reach direction. The apparent M1 execution advantage reflects stronger sensitivity to measured movement kinematics rather than complementary population organization. Regional differences reflect unequal arm or hemisphere composition, or dedicated-versus-distributed bilateral components, rather than PMd–M1 specialization. An apparent crossover results from unmatched information content or discriminability between the preparatory and execution comparisons.
