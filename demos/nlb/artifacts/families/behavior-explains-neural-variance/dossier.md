# Behavioral-state explanations for motor-cortical population variability

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Reclassifies apparently unexplained M1 and PMd variability by testing multidimensional behavioral-state explanations, with distinct regional-distribution and task-subspace-overlap variants.

## Scientific Motivation

Population variability may reflect intrinsic neural dynamics or latent readiness, but it may also encode measured movement, gaze, or other embodied state; predictability alone does not determine its scientific meaning.

## Variant Plans

- Variant 1 (regional distribution test): Is population variability associated with multidimensional behavioral state distributed similarly across PMd and M1, or selectively concentrated in one region?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant asks where behavior-associated variability is distributed across M1 and PMd, not whether it aligns with reach-task geometry. The discriminating observation is Reliable behavior-associated population structure in both regions versus selective structure in one region, evaluated against simpler state summaries and matched reliability checks, would distinguish distributed from localized accounts.
  - Planned analysis: The documented behavioral modalities and corrected region-assignment rule support a matched PMd-versus-M1 analysis of behavior-associated population structure, with single-session and measurement limitations retained. Refined question: Within the documented MC_Maze_Small training session, is neural population structure associated with multidimensional measured behavioral state comparably detectable in PMd and M1 after matched reliability and task-condition checks? Population and scope: Sorted units assigned to PMd and M1 by the documented unit-ID convention, and successful training trials from the one macaque delayed reaching session; no test trials are used. Data sources: Training NWB behavioral time series, trial descriptors, and sorted-unit event data, with region assignment audited using release-specific documentation.; Expected grain: Time bins nested in task epochs and trials, with units nested in cortical region within one recording session.; Required variables: Hand position and velocity, Cursor position, Eye position, Trial timing and maze or target descriptors, Sorted-unit spike times and unit IDs; Limitations: Only measured movement and gaze are available; latent readiness and other unmeasured state remain competing explanations. The release is one subject and one session. Unit of observation: Cross-validated, event-aligned time-bin population activity paired with contemporaneous and lagged measured behavioral features. Unit of inference: Region-specific held-out trial blocks within the one session; region comparison is a within-session associational contrast. Hierarchy and dependence: Preserve trial blocks during all splitting and resampling; model temporal autocorrelation with blocked folds and aggregate comparisons at the trial-block level rather than treating bins as independent. Analysis strategy: Construct a multidimensional behavioral-state feature set from hand position, hand velocity, cursor position, eye position, and prespecified lags, standardized within training folds. For each region, estimate cross-validated behavior-associated population dimensions using a regularized reduced-rank encoding model or canonical latent-variable model with dimensions selected only by inner-fold stability. Compare held-out explained neural variance or aligned latent reliability using matched unit-count subsampling, matched trial blocks, and uncertainty intervals over blocked resamples. Compare the full behavioral state against simpler kinematic-only and gaze-only summaries without choosing features from target outcomes. Candidate estimands: Difference between PMd and M1 in held-out fraction of population activity variance associated with the prespecified measured behavioral-state feature set. Difference between regions in reliability of independently estimated behavior-associated population dimensions after matching unit count and trial blocks. Validation strategy: Verify timestamp alignment and trial coverage before fitting; use fold-separated preprocessing, within-fold dimension selection, label and time-shift controls, and recovery on synthetic data with known regional equality or selectivity. Split strategy: Use outer blocked trial folds stratified by documented task descriptors; retain whole trials and contiguous bins in one fold, with inner blocked folds for regularization and dimension selection. Diagnostics: Per-fold availability and missingness of each behavioral modality. Region assignment audit using unit IDs and the documented electrode-index correction caveat. Region-wise unit count, firing-coverage, and behavioral-coverage summaries without interpreting them as neural findings. Stability of dimensions and estimands across trial blocks, lags, and matched-unit resamples. Negative controls: Circularly time-shift behavioral features within intact trial blocks beyond the prespecified lag window. Permute behavioral-feature blocks across matched trials while preserving region and task labels. Positive controls: Recover known simulated behavior-to-population coupling and known equal-versus-selective regional structure using the planned blocked validation procedure. Alternative explanations: Unequal unit yield, firing reliability, or region-label conversion error. Task-condition imbalance or reach-dependent behavior rather than general behavioral state. Unmeasured state, recording drift, and temporal autocorrelation. Predicted result patterns: Comparable reliable behavior-associated structure in both regions after matching reliability would support the distributed associational account. A stable regional difference surviving matching and controls would support differentiated organization within this session. Failure of stability or control checks would leave regional organization unresolved rather than support an intrinsic-variability account. Why this serves the question: It preserves the regional-distribution contrast by estimating the same measured behavioral-state construct independently in PMd and M1 and treating reliability, region labels, and task covariation as explicit alternatives rather than collapsing the question into task-subspace overlap. - Variant 2 (task-state geometric overlap test): Do behavioral-state-associated neural dimensions overlap with or remain geometrically distinct from dimensions distinguishing reach demands?
  - Distinguishing test: This variant tests the relationship between behavioral-state and reach-demand dimensions; it can show overlap or separation regardless of their regional distribution. The discriminating observation is Cross-validated alignment between independently defined behavioral and reach-demand dimensions, compared with matched controls and condition-balanced behavior, would distinguish integration from separability.
  - Planned analysis: The documented behavioral signals and reach-task fields support independently defined behavioral and reach-demand subspaces, provided the proxy definition and condition-balancing rule are reviewed before execution. Refined question: Within the documented MC_Maze_Small training session, do independently defined neural dimensions associated with measured behavioral state align with, or remain distinct from, dimensions distinguishing prespecified reach demands? Population and scope: All documented training-session sorted units analyzed as a combined population and, secondarily, by documented region; successful training trials only from the one macaque session. Condition support must be checked before a geometry comparison is interpretable. Unit of observation: Cross-validated event-aligned time-bin population activity with independently fitted behavioral-state features and trial-level reach-demand design variables. Unit of inference: Held-out trial blocks within the one session, summarized with uncertainty across blocked resamples. Hierarchy and dependence: Keep all bins from a trial in the same fold; define behavioral and reach-demand subspaces in separate training partitions and evaluate alignment only on held-out blocks to prevent circular geometry estimates. Analysis strategy: Define behavioral-state dimensions from continuous hand, velocity, cursor, and eye signals using training-fold-only regularized encoding or reduced-rank methods. Define reach-demand dimensions independently from target geometry plus the prespecified maze or barrier descriptors, subject to Owner confirmation of this proxy. Estimate held-out subspace alignment with principal angles or cross-validated shared-variance measures, using matched dimensionality and independently estimated bases. Repeat after balancing or residualizing behavior within reach-demand strata and compare against task-label and behavior time-shift controls. Candidate estimands: Held-out alignment between independently estimated behavioral-state-associated and reach-demand-associated neural subspaces at matched dimensionality. Change in held-out subspace alignment after balancing or residualizing measured behavior within reach-demand strata. Validation strategy: Audit condition counts and modality coverage before fitting; use nested blocked folds, synthetic recovery of known overlapping and orthogonal subspaces, and null calibration with fold-preserving label permutations. Split strategy: Use outer trial-block folds stratified where feasible by documented maze and target descriptors; estimate each subspace only in the relevant training partition and reserve outer folds for alignment evaluation. Diagnostics: Coverage table for target and maze descriptors by outer fold. Behavioral missingness and range overlap across reach-demand strata. Subspace dimensionality stability, basis reliability, and sensitivity to matched dimensionality. Comparison of combined-population and region-stratified sensitivity analyses without treating either as confirmation. Negative controls: Permute reach-demand labels across matched trial blocks within prespecified strata. Time-shift continuous behavioral features within trial blocks beyond the prespecified lag window. Positive controls: Recover known overlap and known orthogonality from synthetic population data using the same cross-validated alignment pipeline. Alternative explanations: Reach-dependent movement or gaze differences induce apparent subspace overlap. Unequal task-condition coverage or limited trial count destabilizes the reach-demand basis. Shared temporal structure, dimensionality mismatch, or unit sampling causes apparent alignment. Predicted result patterns: Stable above-null held-out alignment that remains after behavioral balancing would support an associational integration account. Stable near-null alignment with reliable individual subspaces would support separable task and measured-state components. Loss of reliability or disappearance after balancing would indicate that the proposed geometric distinction is not resolved by this release.
