# Temporal organization of motor population geometry

This is a development planning dossier. It is planning-only and does not report scientific results.
Dataset grounding level: `schema_metadata_inspected`.
Dataset claim status: `unverified`.

## Question Family

Asks whether population geometry during delayed reaching reflects a continuous evolution of one movement representation or a qualitative reorganization between preparation and execution.

## Scientific Motivation

Similar reach behavior could arise from a continuously evolving population trajectory or from distinct preparatory and execution-related organizations; descriptive geometry alone cannot distinguish these accounts.

## Competing Explanations And Controls

Competing explanations that must remain visible, by variant: Variant 1: Apparent cross-phase preservation is explained by measured hand or cursor kinematics, movement timing, or peri-movement activity rather than task-condition organization. Phase-specific geometries encode execution features well within phase but do not preserve relationships among task conditions across phases. Partial overlap between preparatory and execution manifolds creates apparent similarity without a common task-relevant relational geometry. Temporal autocorrelation or dimensional reduction makes phase-local structures appear relationally invariant. Variant 2: Preparatory and execution patterns are geometrically separable, but their behavioral relations evolve smoothly within one continuous regime. A rapid condition-invariant signal related to movement onset or reaction time creates an apparent boundary without changing movement-specific functional meaning. Measured hand or cursor kinematics account for the apparent transition from prospective to contemporaneous information. Regionally or neuronally staggered continuous changes appear abrupt after population aggregation or limited temporal sampling. Pooling different trial contexts produces an artificial switch in predictive relationships.
