# What prior-art review found, and which questions it stopped

Before a question may be planned it is checked against the published record. This page is every version that check did not let through, with the work it ran into.

A stop here is not a verdict on the dataset and not a fault. It is the review doing its job: the questions that got through are the ones that survived it, and reading this page is how you tell that claim from an assertion.

Every record named below resolves to a DOI. A model's impression that something exists is not enough to stop a question; the prior has to resolve to an identity a reader can open. The prose under each one is the reviewer's own account of the overlap, not a summary written afterwards.

## Localized versus distributed organization of reach planning and execution — Temporal-scope test of regional preparation-to-execution organization

Deferred: published work sits close enough that the question would have to be made distinguishable from it first. Not the same question, and not a free one.

**Why.** A content-bearing, DOI-identified PMd–M1 study already compares choice-related population dynamics under predictable versus temporally uncertain stimulus durations and reports an earlier/stronger PMd decision-variable signal only under predictable timing, with PMd and M1 becoming largely indistinguishable under temporal uncertainty. The proposed question therefore needs to make its substantive increment explicit: whether independently event-aligned preparation-to-execution geometry supplies evidence beyond that established timing-context effect, rather than re-testing the reported PMd–M1 ordering result.

**Compared against 2 records:**

- [10.1101/283960](https://doi.org/10.1101/283960)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both address population-level temporal differences between PMd and M1 during perceptual choice coupled to arm movement, especially whether an apparent PMd lead is retained when timing conditions change.
  - *Same tension.* The prior explicitly distinguishes decision-variable dynamics from trivially earlier motor-kinematic signals and finds that temporal uncertainty alters the apparent PMd–M1 difference. This directly overlaps the proposal's concern that a PMd lead might reflect choice timing or context-dependent latency rather than fixed motor staging.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior contrasts PMd with M1 in monkeys performing a direction-discrimination arm-report task and compares fixed/predictable with variable/uncertain stimulus timing. The candidate uses the same regional and timing-context contrast, while adding preparation-to-execution mappings and neural-event alignment.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* The prior reports stronger and earlier PMd decision variables under fixed predictable timing but largely indistinguishable PMd and M1 dynamics under temporal uncertainty. The candidate's proposed loss of stable ordering under uncertainty is thus an already reported discriminating temporal observation; its additional proposed discriminator is cross-region correspondence versus differentiation of preparation-to-execution mappings after event alignment.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior's convergence under uncertainty supports a context-sensitive account of PMd–M1 timing rather than an invariant PMd-first ordering. The candidate assigns essentially that same implication to disappearance of ordering, but further proposes that event-aligned mapping geometry could distinguish staging from distributed transformation.
- [10.1038/ncomms13239](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13239)
  - *Same phenomenon.* Both concern the relation between preparatory and movement-period population activity and whether preparation and execution are separate but linked population computations.
  - *Same tension.* The prior establishes orthogonal preparatory and movement subspaces with lawful relations between them, which bears on the candidate's premise that preparation-to-execution mappings can be informative about transformation organization.
  - *Same contrast.* The prior concerns motor cortex during reaching and does not provide the candidate's PMd-versus-M1 comparison, choice-formation separation, or temporal-predictability/uncertainty contrast.
  - *Same discriminating observation.* It provides a within-population preparation-to-movement subspace reorganization and lawful linkage, but does not test whether cross-region mappings are matched or region-specific, nor whether PMd–M1 event-relative ordering persists across timing contexts.
  - *Same outcome meaning.* The prior shows that distinct preparatory and movement subspaces need not imply unlinked computations. Accordingly, evidence of differentiated mappings alone would not uniquely establish sequential PMd-to-M1 staging without a cross-region and timing-context-specific prediction.
